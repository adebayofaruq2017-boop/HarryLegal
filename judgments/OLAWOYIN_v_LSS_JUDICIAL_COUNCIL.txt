RATIO DECIDENDI
The word 'shall' used in Section 35(4) of the LSS Constitution 2024 conveys a mandatory obligation with respect to delivering judgments to parties, but is interpreted as a directory concerning the public of such judgments on notice boards and dissemination to other LSS organs. Failure to give judgment to parties violates constitutional rights and the principles of natural justice.

JUDGMENT OF HON. JUSTICE ISMAIL KHADIJAT MOROMOKE (PRESIDING
JUSTICE)
For the just determination of this suit, this honourable court deems it fit to couch asole issue which is;
Whether or not the word “shall” in Section 35(4) of the Law Students’ Society Constitution 2024 appearing twice in the said section connotes mandatoriness
which the defendant has breached?
For ease of interpretation, it is important to reproduce Section 35(4) of the Law Students’ Society Constitution 2024. It states;
“the ruling of the judiciary SHALL, upon being delivered, be given to parties and be published on the society’s board and a copy SHALL be given to the various organs of the society within five school days”.
In Adams v. Umar(2009) 5 NWLR (Pt. 1133) 41, the court of appeal held that in its ordinary meaning, “shall” is a word of command which must be given an obligatory meaning denoting compulsion. It has the invaluable consequence of excluding the thought of discretion to impose a duty which may be enforced.
In Ashaolu Samuel Funmilayo v The President of the Students’ Union and 2 Ors (2023)4 USLR with suit no: SUC/UIL/JC/FI/007/2023, this honourable court held in that case that, “ the word shall is used to express command or exhortation or what is legally mandatory.
  78

Also, in Honeywell Flour Mills Plc v. Ecobank (Nig) Ltd (2016) 16 NWLR (Pt. 1539) 387, the word “shall” in its ordinary meaning is a word of command which is normally given a compulsory meaning as it is intended to denote obligation. [Amadi v. N.N.P.C. (2000) 10 NWLR (Pt.674) 76; Mbang v. Guardian Newspapers Ltd. (2010) LPELR (4479) referred to. However, in Adisa v. Teno Engineering Ltd.(2001) 1 NWLR (Pt. 695) 633, the court held that ‘the word “shall” is not always used to denote command. It may be used in its mandatory or discretionary sense. Whether the word is intended to convey mandatory action or simply directory depends on the general tenor of the statute or legal instrument in which it is used particularly in the section of the law where it is made to appear’.
In addition, the court in Mekwunye v. Carnation Registrars Ltd. (2021) 15 NWLR (Pt. 1798) 1, held that ‘the word “shall” does not always mean “must”, a matter of compulsion. It could be interpreted, where the context so admits, to mean “may”. Whereas the word “may” is not always “may”. It may sometimes be equivalent to “shall”. [Ifezue v. Mbadugha (1984) SCNLR 427.
In Abiodun v. A.-G., Federation (2007) 15 NWLR (Pt. 1057) 359, it is held that “it is also the right of an accused person before it to be given, on demand, a copy of the judgment of the court/tribunal within seven days of the conclusion of the case. This is a right guaranteed by the 1979 Constitution [now 1999 Constitution of Federal Republic of Nigeria, Section 36(7)] for every citizen of this country. Therefore nothing and no one must be allowed to derogate from it. One of the reasons why it becomes imperative for a person tried for a criminal offence to be entitled to the record of the court at the conclusion of the trial is just so that he can exercise his right to appeal to a higher court against that decision if he so wishes. Again, the 1979 Constitution) provides for appeals from courts as well as from tribunals.
The defendant being the Judicial structure of the faculty of law and by Section 15(5)(d) of the Students’ Union Constitution is a court from which appeals can arise from to this honorable court hence, the need for the copy of judgement to be served to the parties in a suit concluded at the LSS court as a constitutional right to be able to file an appeal, if necessary. Looking at the 9-paragraph affidavits of both Claimants as well as the 12-paragraph affidavit of the defendants’ counsel, it can be seen that the major contention is with regards to the publishing of the court judgement on the LSS’s notice board which the employing of the word, “shall” that it connotes mandatoriness and compulsion. This honourable court, however, will determine the issue holistically.
As regards the first “SHALL” in Section 35(4) of the LSS Constitution 2024 as it relates to parties, it is a command and must be compulsorily adhered to by the LSS Judiciary. Parties must be given the copy of the ruling of the court judgement within five working days being a constitutional right in line with the case of Abiodun v. A.G., Federation (2007) 15 NWLR (Pt. 1057) 359 since right of appeal is not only limited to criminal matters alone. In fact in Tsokwa v Ibi (2017)10 NWLR (pt. 1574)343, the court of appeal held that, “appeal is a constitutional right which cannot be taken away from or denied an appellant. No court of law has the jurisdiction to take away from or deny an appellant his constitutional right of appeal. Also in Oruche v C.O.P. (1997)4 NWLR (pt.497)1, the supreme Court held that, “by virtue of Section 258(1) of the 1979 Constitution (as amended) [now Section 294(1) of Constitution
79

of Federal Republic of Nigeria 1999 As Amended], it is mandatory for the court to deliver copies of its judgement, authenticated, to parties within seven days of its delivery.”
To the Second “SHALL” in the said section, as regards providing other organs of theLSS under Section 9 of the LSS Constitution 2024 with the copies of the rulings and judgements of the LSS Judicial Council as well as publishing same on the LSS notice board is merely an administrative duty which the lower courts are enjoined to carry out for ease of practice in the LSS jurisdiction..
This honorable court grant the first prayer of the claimants in this case only with modification which is a DECLARATION OF THIS HONORABLE COURT that the defendant has a constitutional obligatory duty vested on her pursuant to Section 35 (4) of the Law Students’ Society Constitution 2024 as regards providing parties to a concluded suit with the copy of the court ruling and/or judgement within five school working days while publishing same on the LSS notice board and giving same to other organs of LSS is mere directory and administrative duty to be carried out by the LSS Judiciary to ease legal practice in the LSS jurisdiction. The 9-paragraph affidavits of the Claimants do not, in any way, reveal any substantial and personalinjury suffered, this honorable court refused to grant any order as to cost.
