RULING ON THE DEFENDANT‟S PRELIMINARY OBJECTION 
This ruling will be premised on the issue one has couched by the defendants that; 
Whether or not this suit is academic? 
After a proper consideration of the preliminary objection filed by the Defendant in this suit and the reply 
of the Claimant to the preliminary objection, this Honourable Court hereby proceeds to its ruling, in order 
to determine the PO, it is pertinent to consider judicial pronouncement on the academic suit, Court in th e 
case of PLATEAU STATE V. A .G. FED. supra has held that academic suit is the one that is devoid of 
any live issue or question, the determination of which will not offer any progressive benefit whatsoever to 
our jurisprudence and legal development within our jurisdiction. This is also the position in the cases of 
DANAL V. SALEH (2015) 2 NWLR [1444] 259. ODOM V. PDP (2015) 6 NWLR [1456] 527 @ 570 . 
Notwithstanding the above, His Lordship, Per Legbo Kutigi CJN (as he then was) held in the case of 
PLATEAU STATE supra that “issues (or/and questions) relating to the i nterpretation of constitution 
which is a living document are serious issues (or/and questions) and cannot be regarded as academic, 
speculative and hypothetical. So also is the issue (and/or question) of a declaration of a state of 
emergency”. In this instance case, the bone of contention is the interpretation of art. 4(3) of constitution 
of HRC.  This is clear on the surface of claimant‟s claims and reliefs. As it has been argued by the 
Claimant‟s counsel, the office of the A. G. of HRC is the creation of co nstitution of the Chambers. See 
section 4(3) constitution of HRC . On this basis, the office of A. G. HRC is expected to be in service at 
any material time because its creator which is the constitution is living document that is immutable.  
On the strength of Para 6 of Claimant‟s affidavit in support of the Originating summon and paragraphs 5 -
10 of the Defendant‟s affidavit in support of the Preliminary Objection, this cause of action has accrued 
before nullification of the first appointment into the office of the A G. of HRC which lasted from 
November 2019 to January 2020. This period is enough if not more than enough to allege breach of 

8 |  P a g e
 
constitutional duty notwithstanding the nullification. This is because this alleged constitutional wrong has 
been accruing until nullification of the appointment and it is living wrong. As it is held in PLATEAU 
STATE CASE, the fact that this wrong has passed does not make the matter academic but a constitutional 
wrong that can be challenged at any material time. This case is not academic and cannot be academic suit. 
At this juncture, this honourable court agrees with Defence‟s counsel to the extent that academic suit 
cannot be entertained by this court but this court holds that constitutional matter cannot be a n academic 
suit. We as well hold that this honourable court has jurisdiction to entertain this case. We hereby resolve 
the PO against the DC and in the favour of the CC. The preliminary objection of the defendant is hereby 
dismissed in its entirety.

RULING ON THE DEFENDANT‟S PRELIMINARY OBJECTION 
Having listened to both sides on merit, this Honourable Court proceeds to give the following ruling. For 
easy dispensation of this ruling, a sole issue was couched as follows; 
Whether or not the Article 4 of the Constitution of Human Rights Chamber (2018) is justiciable? 
The question of justiciability of certain sections of Nigerian Constitution is one of the constitutional 
questions that have captured the  attention of legal luminaries. Many luminaries have registered their 
displeasure towards ousting the jurisdiction of court to entertain any issues that bothers on those sections 
in Chapter II of the Nigerian Constitution. The Chapter II of the Nigerian Co nstitution entails fantastic 
provision for the fundamental objectives and directive principles of state policy. However, the pursuant to 
section 6(6)c of the same constitution , the chapter has been fenced out of the jurisdictional competence 
of any courts of law despite its fundamental nature to progress of welfare and affairs of Nigerians. Non -

11 |  P a g e
 
justiciability of the chapter II has been judicially pronounced upon in the cases of A. G. ONDO V. A. G. 
FED. (2002) 9 NWLR [772], OKOGIE V. A. G. LAGOS (1981) NCLR 2187. 
In this instance case, the applicant in his written address in support of the PO, contended that Art. 4 of 
Contitution of HRC has similitude with Chapter II of Nigerian Constitution particularly on education 
which has been adjudged not to be justiciable. On this basis, M. A. Omopupa of counsel argued that art. 4 
of Constitution of HRC is not justiciable. While Q. A Adedimeji of Counsel argued contrary on the 
position that the fact that „fundamental objectives‟ of Nigerian Constitution is not justiciab le does not 
allow persons or authorities to render any other constitutional provision under the tag „fundamental 
objectives‟ as non -justiciable. Upon hearing both sides‟ contentions, this honourable court perused the 
Constitution of HRC. The art. 4 is trul y contained under the title „fundamental objective to the 
constitution and the Chambers‟. However, there is no provision that has the similar legal effect like the 
provision of section of 6(6)c of Nigerian Constitution. Therefore, M. A. Omopupa is basically arguing the 
non-justiciabilty of Art. 4 with Chapter II of Nigerian constitution on the basis that they serve the same 
purpose. But that is not the case in entirety. The fact that both fundamental objectives under Constitution 
of HRC and Nigeria Constitution have the similar purposes does not extend the same legal implication to 
them. As much as Section 6(6)c renders Chapter II of Nigerian Constitution non -justiciable same is not 
the case with art. 4.  
Assuming but not conceding that Constitution of HRC i ntends to render art. 4 non-justiciable, it lacks the 
power to so do, This is the basis that the said constitution is the creation of members of Human Right 
Chambers which is established under LSS constitution. See section 40 of LSS Constitution. By virtue of 
section 7(3) and (4) of LSS constitution , right of access to LSS court is granted to all aggrieved 
members of the society. Section 35 of LSS constitution granted to LSS Judicial Council unlimited 
jurisdiction on civil and constitutional matters. It is not constitutionally correct to argue that ordinary art. 
4 of a chamber‟s constitution would oust the jurisdiction of this honourable court simply because its 
provisions have similarly title and purposes with Chapter II of Nigerian Constitution.  The jurisdiction of 

12 |  P a g e
 
LSS Court cannot be ousted on the basis of incurably bad contention saved there is clear cut provision of 
LSS Constitution that states such. And to the best my knowledge, there is no such provision in the LSS 
Constitution. See DASUKI AHMAD & ANOR V. HUMAN RIGHT CHAMBERS & 3 ORS (2019) 1 
LSSCLR 1.  
Therefore, it is held that art. 4 is justicable. We settle the issue against the applicant and in favour of the 
respondent. We as well hold that this honourable  court has jurisdiction to entertain this case. The 
Preliminary Objection is hereby resolved in favour of the Claimant/Repondent and against the 
Applicant/Defendant.

On issue one, the question of the interpretation of the word SHALL than any other, has well traversed the 
entire gamut of the student legal practice both in this court and in the Student Union Court, over and over. 
For the most part, I am not judicially persuaded to embark on a voyage that will unmask the legal 
importation of this court to unfathomable tragedy  on the interpretation of the word SHALL. Hence, the 
word SHALL when it appears in a statutory provision, it naturally denotes obligation or a command and 
gives no room for discretion. It imposes a duty. A peremptory mandate is enjoined. See the following 
authorities, BAMAIYI v. ATTORNEY -GENERAL, FEDERATION & ORS (2001) 12 NWLR (Pt. 
727) 466 @ 497; JIDE ALADEJOBI v. NIGERIA BAR ASSOCIATION (2013) AELR 2034 (SC); 
ADAMS v. UMAR (2009) 5 NWLR (Pt. 1133) 41 ; and the case of CHIEF FRANCIS UCHENNA & 
ORS v. PEOPLES DEMOCRATIC PARTY & ORS (2013) AELR 2277 (CA). 
Therefore, the courts have emphasized that, in its ordinary meaning SHALL is a word of command which 
must be given an obligatory meaning as denoting compulsion and has the invaluable consequence of 
excluding the thought of discretion of impose a duty which must be enforced. See the cases of SALAM

ABDULSALAM v. THE EXECUTIVE, LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY & ANOR SUIT NO: 
SUJ/001/18-19; SOLIU HAFEEZ ISHOLA v. THE SENATE PRESIDENT OF THE STUDENT 
UNION & ORS SUJ/002/18 -19; OMOPUPA MUHAMMED AWWAL v. PUBLIC RELATIONS 
OFFICER 1, HUMAN RIGHTS CHAMBER (2019) 1 LSSCLR PAGE 8 @ 12; MUHAMMED 
SESAN ISMAILA v. THE JUDICIARY, LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY (2019) 1 LSSCLR PAGE 
69 @ 78 
I thereby chiefly resolve that, the word SHALL as containe d under Article 4(3) of the Constitution of 
HRC (2018) is be construe as mandatory and therefore, it mandates the Attorney General, Human Rights 
Chamber to compulsorily perform the duties spelt out therein, and without exercising any extraneous 
discretion in that regard. 
 
On issue two, this issue seems enormously conjunctive with issue one as settled above. In effect, the 
defendant in this case is bound by the provision of Article 4(3) of the Constitution of HRC (2018) and 
must not attempt any exercise of discretion whatsoever. Flowing from paragraphs 6 & 7 of the Claimant‟s 
affidavit, it expresses that the defendant has not released any publication on law tips and other legal 
matter since the 24th November, 2019. 
At this juncture, it is possible to examine the provision of the said Article 4(3) of the Constitution of HRC 
as follows; 
The office of the Attorney General shall at least release publications on law tips and other legal related 
matter weekly 
Although, justice delayed is justice denied, but it is ce rtain that justice hurried is justice buried. Hence, 
before the question of whether the constitution has been breached or violated, it is handy to belief that, the 
office of the Attorney General of Human Rights Chamber is a constitutionally created office.  See Article 
9(5) of the Constitution of HRC (2018) . By the case of THE GOVERNOR OF OYO STATE v. 

16 |  P a g e
 
HON. JUSTICE IYABO YERIMA & ANOR (2014) AELR 5400 (CA),  it follows that the Attorney 
General, HRC is a corporation sole recognized by the Constitution and the law of the land as having a 
personality distinct and different from separate individual holder for the time being of that office. 
At large, I align myself with the position of Claimant that the vacancy of the office of the Attorney 
General of HRC does not vitiate the existence and duties of the office. However, I must say that the toga 
of juris personae conferred on the office must only remain a legal abstraction. The office itself has no 
mind of its own any more than it has a body of its own. Its active and directive will, must consequently be 
sought in person of who is occupying the office, the very ego and center of Attorney General of HRC. 
Therefore, proper justice demands the consideration of whether the office of the Attorney General of 
HRC is indeed vacant at the material time in this suit. Earlier before this case, this court gave a decision in 
the case of AJADI v. HUMAN RIGHTS CHAMBER [Judicial Notice] which was commenced on 
21st November 2019. 
It is within the knowledge of this court that, the decis ion in the above decision reaffirmed that the office 
of Attorney General of HRC is not vacant and occupied in that wise. This was the position until 30th of 
January, 2020 when the decision of the superior court in KEHINDE v. THE LORD ACTIVIST, 
HUMAN RIGHTS  CHAMBER & ANOR SUJ/UIL/AP/002/19 -20, declared the office as vacant. 
Consequent upon the mathematical analysis, it is obvious to me that, the defendant cannot escape its 
duties and constitutional duties within the ample period when it is being occupied. In  effect, there is no 
concrete evidence before this court satisfying that the defendant did observe the provision of Article 4(3) 
of the Constitution HRC (2018) by releasing publications on law tips and other legal related matter 
within those periods. This is a flagrant violation of the constitution. See paragraphs 6 & 7 of the 
Claimant's affidavit. I am in concession with the notion that the defendant merely needs sufficient time to 
perform his duty and it does not matter that the office was occupied for on ly one week or two. For 
academic reasons, the Article 4 (3) raises a fundamental duty to advance the academic and legal prowess 
of the students of law and to keep them informed. Therefore, I have no other option as legally secured 

17 |  P a g e
 
rather than to hold that the defendant has breached the provision of Article 4(3) of the Constitution of 
HRC. This issue is settled in favour of the Claimant. 
Upon the foregoing reasoning, I hereby grant only the 1st, 2nd, 3rd and 4th prayers of the Claimant in this 
case and all other prayers sought by the parties to this suit are hereby refused.
