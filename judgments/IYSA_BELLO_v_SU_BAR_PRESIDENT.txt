COURT
I have had the opportunity to read the judgment delivered by my learned brother, Hon. Justice Muhammad Kamaldeen Gemisola and I concur with his well articulated position. The crux of the matter lies in whether or not there was a breach of the duty stipulated in Section 7(1)(A) of the Students’ Union Bar Constitution 2017 (as amended) under overriding and uncontrollable circumstances (force majeure). The defendant's argument hinged on the premise that unforeseen circumstances, namely the Call to Bar ceremony ,the faculty renovations, and the Dean's directives, prevented the President and the Executive of the Students’ Union Bar from fulfilling their constitutional duty to convene a general meeting for the month of April. However, the doctrine of force majeure, as clearly established in our jurisdiction is intrinsically tied to contractual relationships. It operates to excuse parties from performance when an external, unforeseeable event beyond their control makes the performance of a contractual obligation impossible. See the case of Union Bank of Nigeria v. Ozigi (1994) 3 NWLR (Pt. 931) 385, where the Supreme Court held that force majeure clauses are to be interpreted strictly and apply only to what the parties agreed upon. The Students’ Union Bar Constitution is not a contract. It is the main rulebook for the association, as stated by Section A(1)(B) of the University of Ilorin Student Legal Practitioner Act of the Students’ Union Compendium "The Association shall manage its affairs by its own constitution." This constitution sets out duties that must be performed. In my view, force majeure cannot be applied to constitutional duties unless the constitution itself makes provision for such, as it would weaken our rules and allow individuals to selectively comply with constitutional duties. However, even if force majeure could be applicable in this instance, the provisions of the Students’ Union Bar Constitution are clear and unambiguous, particularly Section 7(1)(A). It does not mandate a physical venue for the general meeting, meaning that alternative platforms or forms can be employed to fulfill this constitutional duty. The law does not command the impossible, but it equally does not excuse a lack of innovation or effort in fulfilling a clear duty.
In view of this, I hereby concur entirely with the judgment of my learned brother on the position that the constitutional duty of the Students' Union Bar, as stipulated in Section 7(1)(A) of the 2017 (2018 as amended) Constitution has been breached and the constitution should be strictly followed and complied with
