Defendant  RATIO DECIDENDI 1. ACTION: CAUSE OF ACTION AND RIGHT OF ACTION – ON THE DIFFERENCE BETWEEN CAUSE OF ACTION AND RIGHT OF ACTION. It is intrinsically mounted on cause of action. It has been defined in galaxy of authorities to mean fact or set of facts that warrant a party to approach court for redress. See EGBE V. ADEFARASIN supra. However, court in FASEYE V. AG FEDERATION (2015) 16 NWLR (1485)261@ 267, held that "a cause of action constitutes a set of facts or fact which gives a person a right to claim a judicial redress where he is wronged. A right of action on the other hand entitles him to maintain an action on the wrong alleged with a view to getting a judicial redress." In this case, cause of action and right of action have been distinguished, that is for any successful cause of action, it must be accompanied with right of action, otherwise the action will fail. 2. ADMINISTRATIVE LAW: DUTY OF CHAMBERS – ON THE DUTY OF CHAMBERS IN EASING BURDEN OF EXECUTIVE COUNCIL. To my mind! The creation of Chambers is to ease the burden on the executive council in the course of discharging the duty of working and improving on the advocacy skill of the 179 members in the society. Constitutionally, there are media to ensure the compliance with this constitutional mandate. 3. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: THE DOCTRINE OF SEPARATION OF POWER – THE PURPORT OF THE DOCTRINE OF SEPARATION OF POWERS. By way of definition, separation of power is aspect of constitutional law in which we are made to understand that every arm of government has their clearly constitutional duties or gardens. And any attempt to go outside the pristine of the said garden will be ultra vires, nullity, impracticable and of no effect. In that regard, every arm must always wear serious face in their constitutionally ordained duties without peeping at the other arms’ garden. In EMMANUEL GBADEBO OLUSI & ANOR V. CLEMENT SUNDAY OBANOBI & ANOR, suit no. CA/B/309/2008, Court held that “the principle of separation is that none of the three arms of government under the constitution should encroach into the powers of the other.” Reliance is also based on TORKUMA UGBAH & ANOR V. GABRIEL TORWUA SUSWAN & ORS, SUIT NO. SC.191-191A/2012, GOYANG KAYILI V. ESLY YILBUK & ORS SC.92/2005, AG. ABIA V. AG, FEDERATION. (2003) 4 NWLR (PT. 809) 124. 4. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: THE PRINCIPLE OF THE OUSTER CLAUSE – OPERATION OF THE OUSTER CLAUSE PROVISIONS.  

 3 
Ouster clauses are also another constitutionally enshrined principle that curtails or divests the jurisdiction of court from entertaining clearly stipulated matters, if it is brought before the court. Despite the clear provisions of ouster clauses in the constitution of federal republic of Nigeria 1999 [as amended], courts have been saddled with the mandate of ensuring strict compliance with condition precedent. Impliedly, court can still entertain matters being ousted, not because of anything but for compliance with constitutional provision. See the case of INAKOJU V. ADELEKE (2007) Vol II MJSC pg. 233, Sir JUDE AGBASO V. SPEAKER IMO STATE HOUSE OF ASSEMBLY (CA/OW/291/2013). Court has held in plethora of cases that ouster clauses in any constitutional provision must be clear, direct, and explicit and not implicit. That is, it must be clearly stated and the matter being ousted must also be clearly enshrined in the constitution. It was held in LOCAL GOVT. SERVICE COMMISSION & ANOR V. MR. M. A. JEGEDE (CA/EK/07/M/2013) that “the law is firmly established that if any court and more so appellate court, is to be divested of its conferred jurisdiction, it is done expressly and not impliedly. In a similar vein, a court of law can only be garbed or clothed with the toga of finality by express provision to that effect and not by implication”, also in ROTIMI WILLIAMS AKINTOKUN V. LPDC (SC. 111/2006) court held that ―I am not unaware that in the absence of express and clear provision in the constitution or statute which oust the jurisdiction of the courts, the court should be very reluctant to hold that its jurisdiction has been ousted”. 5. COURT: LAW STUDENT SOCIETY COURT – THE EXTENT OF THE JURISDICTION OF THE LSS COURT. By sec. 35 of LSS CONSTITUTION, this Honourable Court is blessed with unlimited jurisdiction on Constitutional matters (in which the instance case is one) and civil matters couple with other matter therein. Likewise, sec. 37 of LSS Constitution crowns this court power to discipline anybody subject to conditions state in the said section.  6. INTERPRETATION OF STATUTE: CANONS OF INTERPRETATION – LITERAL RULE OF INTERPRETATION OF STATUTE  It is trite principle of law, when the content and provision of law is clear, apt and unambiguous, court should not embark on voyage of discovery to input what is not contained therein, but the ordinary and literal meaning should be deployed, see INAKOJU’S CASE supra 7. WORDS AND PHRASE:  CONDITION PRECEDENT  Condition precedent has been settled in myriads of authorities to affect the jurisdiction of the court when it is not fulfilled and makes any resultant action unripe for hearing. We place reliance on LAFIA LOCAL GOVT. V. GOVT. NASARAWA ST. (2012) 17 NWLR (1328) 94 SC. N.U.R.T.W. vs. R.T.E.A.N. (2012)10 NWLR (1307) 170 SC. BAWA V ALIYU (2015) 3 NWLR (1447) 523@ 527, EMEKA V. OKADIGBO (2012) 18 NWLR (1331) 55 SC. 8. WORDS AND PHRASE – JURISDICTION – MEANING OF JURISDICTION.

JUDGE ABDULHAMEED AYATULLAH JLSS)   RULING ON 3RD DEFENDANT PRELIMINARY OBJECTION IN RESPONSE TO THE CLAIMANTS ORIGINATING SUMMON  The preliminary objection was brought in response to claimant originating summon dated 4th of May 2018. In the originating summon in which claimants applied for the interpretation of the word 'shall' contained in sec. 40 of LSS Constitution (Amended) 2017, and whether or not the said provision has been breached by the defendants. They also sought 8 prayers to be granted by the court.   On 15th of May 2018, 3rd Defendant filed a preliminary objection (herein called PO) to the claimants' originating summon challenging the jurisdiction of the Honorable Court. The PO was mounted on 4 grounds, in the course of argument the grounds was reduced to two main points. On the first stand point, 3rd defendant content that originating process of claimants did not disclose reasonable cause of action while citing DIAMOND PETROLEUM INTERNATIONAL LIMITED V. GOVERNOR OF CBN. (2015)14 NWLR (1478) 179 CA.  He also cited S. 40 of LSS Constitution particularly first, second, third and fourth provisos. They further content that the condition precedent had not been fulfilled while relying on IGBINEDON V. EDO ATATE BOARD OF IR (2017) LPELR-41619 CA. He finally submitted that claimants had no cause of action.  On second stand point, He content that the action of claimants was a breach of ancestrally enshrined principle of separation of powers in the constitution. He further content that the constitution being the grund norm may restrict or expand the powers of each arm of government by express provision in itself. Relying on AG ABIA V. AG FEDERATION (2003)4 NWLR (809)124 inter alia. He also cited sec. 40 of LSS Constitution proviso 4 and referred Honorable Court to prayer 5, 6 and 7 of claimants.

“5- An order of this Honorable Court compelling and mandating the defendants to perform their mandated Constitutional duty impose on them by the provision of s. 40 of LSS Constitution within 14 work days and must not be merged with this semester date  6- An order of this Honorable Court mandating the defendants to tender written apology on the society within three working days after the determination of this suit. 7 - An order of this Honorable Court that the defendants should pay a token of 20,000 as the cost of this litigation.”  He further content that the prayers would cause this Honorable Court to breach the principle of separation of power and submitted on this.  On the 3rd stand point, he content that this Honorable Court lack jurisdiction to entertain this case because the case disclose no reasonable cause of action and the principle of separation of powers would be breached. Relying on MADUKOLU V. NKEMDILIN (1962) 2 SCNLR 31. He hereby submitted that this Honorable Court lacked jurisdiction.   Claimants in their defense to PO of 3rd defendant, they content that there is reasonable cause of action, relying on EGBE V. ADEFARASIN (1987) 1 NWLR (47) 1 @ 20 inter alia and referred to paragraph 9 to 12 of first claimant's affidavit and paragraph 10 to 12 of second defendant's affidavit. They further content that the proviso of sec. 40 of LSS Constitution relied upon by 3rd Defendant was purported to oust the jurisdiction of the court while citing SHELL PETROLEUM DEVELOPMENT COMPANY LTD. V. JOEL ANARO  & ORS suit no. SC. 52/2005, S. 35 and 37 of LSS Constitution, that the mere fact that the executive has power to discipline the erring Chambers did not oust the jurisdiction of the court to do same. They further content that principle of separation of power cannot be breached because sec 35(1) of LSS Constitution granted unlimited jurisdiction on civil and constitutional matters cum sec. 37 of same Constitution embellished court with disciplinary power against anybody. They humbly submitted that the doctrine cannot be breached.   They submitted on the whole that this Honorable Court had jurisdiction to entertain the matter brought before it and pray that the PO should be dismissed.  In the 3rd defendant's Counsel reply on point of law, he posited that he was not saying court did not have jurisdiction but the condition precedent to hear same had not been fulfilled. He cited MADUKOLU's case. He submitted that sec. 40 only seeks to ensure that its conditions have been complied with and not ousting the jurisdiction of the Honorable Court.    RULING

Having listen to both parties on the PO filed by 3rd Defendant's Counsel, for easy determination of this PO, three issues have been carefully couched and they read thus;  1. Whether or not ancestrally enshrine principle of separation of power will be breached?  2. Whether or not claimants have reasonable cause of action?  3. Whether or not this honorable court has jurisdiction to hear this case or same has been ousted?  On issue 1 that bothers on separation of power, it had been content by 3rd Defendant’s counsel that granting the reliefs or prayers of the claimants will cause court to infringe and encroach the constitutional duty and power of other arms of government. However, Claimant’s counsel content that the Defendant’s counsel’s contention will cause ousting the jurisdiction of the court. In that manner, two principles of law are striving for mastery. They are separation of power and ouster clauses. By way of definition, separation of power is aspect of constitutional law in which we are made to understand that every arm of government has their clearly constitutional duties or gardens. And any attempt to go outside the pristine of the said garden will be ultra vires, nullity, impracticable and of no effect. CA/B/309/2008, Court held that “the principle of separation is that none of the three arms of government under the constitution should encroach into the powers of the other.” Reliance is also based on  TORKUMA UGBAH & ANOR V. GABRIEL TORWUA SUSWAN & ORS, SUIT NO. SC.191-191A/2012, GOYANG KAYILI V. ESLY YILBUK & ORS SC.92/2005, AG. ABIA V. AG, FEDERATION.  (2003) 4 NWLR (PT. 809) 124  At the other side, ouster clauses are also another constitutionally enshrined principle that curtails or divests the jurisdiction of court from entertaining clearly stipulated matters, if it is brought before the court. In a similar vein, a court of law can only be garbed or clothed with the toga of finality by express provision to that effect and not by implication”, also in ROTIMI WILLIAMS AKINTOKUN V. LPDC (SC. 111/2006) court held that “I am not unaware that in the absence of express and clear provision in the constitution or statute which oust the jurisdiction of the courts, the court should be very reluctant to hold that its jurisdiction has been ousted”  With and without microscopic scrutiny of the clear content of sec. 40 of LSS constitution (Amended) 2017, it does not disclose any clear provision of ouster clause rather it establishes chambers, awards of financial rights to the chambers, establishes duty for chambers, mode of sanctioning erring chambers and right of members to appear before the judiciary. It means that the section does not have affiliations to ouster clause. On the discipline erring chambers, the provision enshrine principle of separation of power for that, which means no chambers can be sanction saved on the recommendation of executive subject to ratification by two-third majority of the House. In case, if any arm is falling short of noncompliance with this provision, then court can have power to ensure compliance.  The section does not contain any clear-cut provision that said that court cannot entertain any matters arises therefrom like that of section 143 (10) and 188(10) of constitution of federal Republic of Nigeria. It is even near separation and far from being ouster clause. It is trite principle of law, when the content and provision of law is clear, apt and unambiguous, court should not embark on voyage of discovery to input what is not contained therein, but the ordinary and literal meaning should be deployed, see INAKOJU’S CASE supra  With all sense of alacrity, we hereby hold that the claimant action will not only let court climbs beyond its limit but it will waste the precious time, invaluable erudition and energetic strength of this honorable court in the course of breaching principle of separation of powers. In that regard, sec. 40 of LSS constitution is not ouster clause but a constitutional provision that enshrines principle of separation of powers.  On issue 2, it is intrinsically mounted on cause of action. It has been defined in galaxy of authorities to mean fact or set of facts that warrant a party to approach court for redress.  See EGBE V. ADEFARASIN supra. However, court in FASEYE V. AG FEDERATION  (2015) 16 NWLR (1485)261@ 267, held that "a cause of action constitutes a set of facts or fact which gives a person a right to claim a judicial redress where he is wronged. A right of action on the other hand entitles him to maintain an action on the wrong alleged with a view to getting a judicial redress." In this case, cause of action and right of action have been distinguished, that

is for any successful cause of action, it must be accompanied with right of action, otherwise the action will fail.  In the instance case, the paragraph 9-12 of first claimant's affidavit and paragraph 1012 of second claimant's affidavit though disclose cause of action reasonably but it is not accompanied with right of action because their affidavits do not declare violation of any ratified order of the executive by the defendants which will amount to right of action.   Also, granting the claimants cause of action and right of action will be an affront on integrity of the court being defender of separation of powers and upholder of constitutionalism. On this, we hold that claimants have cause of action but they lack right of action to maintain this case before this Honorable Court.  On the third issue! It challenges the jurisdiction of this Honorable Court. Jurisdiction is the principle that garbs and clothes court of law its power to hear and entertain any action brought before it. Lack of jurisdiction will make the court a gathering where Judicial and theatrical rehearsal take place that will add no value to legal development. In other word, any proceeding by any court without jurisdiction is nullity. Reliance is placed on MADINKOLU'S case, INAKOJU's case supra, ELELU-HABEEB V. AG. FED. (2012) 13 NWLR (1318) 423 SC. By sec. 35 of LSS CONSTITUTION, this Honorable Court is blessed with unlimited jurisdiction on Constitutional matters (in which the instance case is one) and civil matters couple with other matter therein. Likewise, sec. 37 of LSS Constitution crowns this court power to discipline anybody subject to conditions state in the said section.  However, in sec. 40 of LSS Constitution, any erring Chambers will only be sanctioned by the Executive council on the ratification by two-third majority of the House of Representatives. Going by literal interpretation, this section does not oust the jurisdiction of this Honorable Court but merely lays down condition precedent to be fulfilled before jurisdiction can be assumed.  To my mind! The creation of Chambers is to ease the burden on the executive council in the course of discharging the duty of working and improving on the advocacy skill of the members in the society. Constitutionally, there are media to ensure the compliance with this constitutional mandate. Although, as alleged by the claimants in their affidavits, the Chambers have wronged in law and in fact not to have performed their constitutional mandate which is the main reason for establishing these Chambers by the constitution.   Nevertheless, the claimants should have followed the media enshrine in the constitution to ensure compliance with this constitutional mandate rather than approaching the court so early. This makes their case unripe for hearing because the condition precedent in the LSS constitution has not been fulfilled. Condition precedent has been settled in myriads of authorities to affect the

jurisdiction of the court when it is not fulfilled and makes any resultant action unripe for hearing. We place reliance on LAFIA LOCAL GOVT. V. GOVT. NASARAWA ST. (2012) 17 NWLR (1328) 94 SC. N.U.R.T.W. vs. R.T.E.A.N. (2012)10 NWLR (1307) 170 SC. BAWA V ALIYU (2015) 3 NWLR (1447) 523@ 527, EMEKA V. OKADIGBO (2012) 18 NWLR (1331) 55 SC. Therefore, we hold that this honorable court lack jurisdiction to entertain unripe matter.   On the whole! We strongly hold that the action of the claimants will drag the court to the garden of other arms of government and the ancestrally enshrined principle of separation of powers will be violated. We also hold that the claimants have reasonable cause of action but they lack right of action to maintain this case before this Honorable Court. Lastly, we hold that this Honorable Court cannot and will not entertain this case because condition precedent in sec. 40 of LSS Constitution has not been fulfilled and it deprives court its jurisdiction. At this juncture, this Honorable Court struck out the originating processes of the claimants for lacking of merit.
