CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: LSS COSNTITUTION – DIFFERENCE BETWEEN LAW STUDENT AND MEMBER OF LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY  It is clear to my mind that the provisions of Section 5 and 6 of the LSS Constitution are very clear; I do not need any art of judicial radicalism to interpret them. As far as our jurisdiction is concerned, there is a line of difference drawn between Law students and members of the Law Students’ Society. In order to assume the initiated into the membership of the Society, one must been admitted as a Law student and matriculated. I suppose I am correct if I say all members of the Society are Law students but not all Law students are members of the Society. It is therefore a condition precedent that for the rights and privileges embedded in the LSS Constitution to be enjoyed, the beneficiary must not only be an admitted student but also matriculated and I so hold. 2. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: LSS COSNTITUTION - QUALIFICATIONS OF MEMBERSHIP OF THE LAW STUDENT SOCIETY  The argument of claimant’s counsel in this line is a poisonous fruit; just a bite will be very disastrous to the health of this court. Section 6 of the LSS Constitution provides that “MEMBERS (bona fide in respect of the Society) means any member who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society. 3. ISSUE 1: WHETHER OR NOT THE APPOINTMENT OF THE 2ND DEFENDANT IS A RIGHT OR A PRIVILEGE. According to the Black’s Law Dictionary (Revised 4thed. Pg. 1487) right means what a man is entitled to have, or to do, or to receive from others within the limit prescribed by law. Privilege is defined by the same dictionary as a particular and peculiar advantage enjoyed by a person, company, or class, beyond the common advantages of other citizens. Section 7 of the LSS Constitution sets out the rights that members of the Society are entitled to and from a thorough and dispassionate perusal of the provision, it is not stated anywhere that appointment to the office of Special Assistant to the Executive President of the Society is a right. If such should be regarded as a right, it means every member of the Society is entitled to be appointed Special Assistant to the President and whoever is not appointed can approach the court for the enforcement of his right putting us in a ridiculous situation where the Executive President of the Society will be having one thousand Special Assistants or more! This is because where there is a legal right equity will not fold its hands and watch it being denied; equity will create a means to vindicate it. Therefore, when no right exists there is nothing to vindicate. The argument of claimant’s counsel In this line is a poisonous fruit; just a bite will be very disastrous to the health of this court. Section 6 of the LSS Constitution provides that “MEMBERS (bona fide in respect of the Society) means any member who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society. 

 10 
“I believe this provision does not, however, give the measure of the word “privileges”. I believe that it is because it may be factually impossible to limit privileges to a particular set of advantages. It is my view that appointment as Special Assistant to the President is one of those privileges; an advantage that can be given to any member of the Society and I so hold. I do not have any difficulty in coming to this conclusion on this issue that appointment of the 2nddefendant by the 1stdefendant is not a right but a privilege subject to the discretionary power of the President to grant to any member of the Society as he so pleases. Issue 1 is hereby resolved in favour of the defendants.    ISSUE 2: WHETHER THE APPOINTMENT OF THE 2ND DEFENDANT BY THE 1ST DEFENDANT IS CONSTITUTIONAL On this issue, claimant was hell-bent that the appointment of the 2nd as Special Assistant to the Executive President of the Law Students’ Society is unconstitutional on the ground that at the time of the appointment the 2nddefendant was only an admitted student and since she was not yet matriculated she was not a member of the Society qualified to be so appointed when Sections 5 and 6 of the LSS Constitution are read together. Here, certain questions are begging for answers such as who Is a Member of the Society? Is there any distinction between a Law student and a member of the Law Students’ Society? I will not answer the questions using intuition but answer them in accordance with the grund norm of this Society. I do not want to be reminded of my duty as a judge; I sit on this bench to interpret the laws of the Society and I do it gleefully. Let me at the expense of verbosity but borne out of necessity quote ipsissima verba necessary provisions of the Sections in question; Section 5 

 11 
“The membership of the society shall consist of duly admitted and matriculated students of the Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin. Section 6 “MEMBERS (bona fide in respect of the Society) means any member who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society.” What makes one a member of the Society? The answer to this is given by this Honourable Court in the case of TAOFIK WALIU OPEYEMI V.THE ELECTORAL COMMISSION, LSS & OTHERS LSSJ/005/19 where per Ibidunmi JLSS (as he then was) held that “by the express mentioning of both admission and matriculation in the provision by the draftsmen surely has the purpose it is supposed to serve. The matriculation oath of the University is customarily Subscribed to on the day of matriculation. It therefore may have been the intention of the lawmaker that while, by admission, the persons to which this section relates may have been students of the University, they shall not be members of the Society without the operation of “and matriculated” which means that they must both be admitted and must have subscribed to the oath of matriculation usually administered on that day.” Matriculation is not just a ceremony; it is more that throwing jamborees as it serves as an important purpose in the academic sojourn of University students. The purpose of the exercise can be garnered from RULE 15 OF THE UNIVERSITY OF ILORIN STUDENT HANDBOOK which provides as follows; “Only students who have satisfied the minimum educational requirements of University of Ilorin are offered final admission and then matriculated as students of the University. On the matriculation day, each fresh student swears to the matriculation oath which shall be provided by the University (SEE APPENDIX B). Violation of the matriculation oath is a very serious offence which shall lead to disciplinary actions.” It is clear to my mind that the provisions of Section 5 and 6 of the LSS Constitution are very clear; I do not need any art of judicial radicalism to interpret them. As far as our jurisdiction is concerned, there is a line of difference drawn between Law students and members of the Law Students’ Society. In order to assume the initiated into the membership of the Society, one must been admitted as a Law student and matriculated. I suppose I am correct if I say all members of the Society are Law students but not all Law students are members of the Society. It is therefore a condition precedent that for the rights and privileges embedded in the LSS Constitution to be enjoyed, the beneficiary must not only be an admitted student but also matriculated and I so hold. 

 12 
My opinion is rooted in the unambiguous and clear provision of Section 6 of the LSS Constitution that a “member” is a person who “…is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society.” When I say Member, I do not mean a buyer or seller in Dugbe market in Ibadan or Ipata market in Ilorin and not a beggar on the street of Lagos, but a member of the Law Students’ Society, University of Ilorin. I hope my unending quest for questions will be forgiven because I still have more to ask; Was the 2nd defendant a Law Student when she was appointed Special Assistant? The answer is Yes. Was she a matriculated student on the day of her appointment? No! Was she a member of the Law Students’ Society when the broadcast message on the 19th July 2021 was made that the 1st defendant appointed her Special Assistant? The answer is again in a big negative! All of these can be garnered from paragraphs 7, 8, 9, and 10 of the uncontroverted affidavit evidence deposed to by the claimant. How is an uncontroverted and unchallenged affidavit treated in law? It is an age old principle that averments in the affidavit of a party which are neither challenged nor controverted by his adversary are deemed admitted and the court must act on those undisputed averments as being true. See YARADUA & ORS V. YANDOMA & ORS SC. 4/2014 PER MUHAMMAD JSC; OWURU & ANOR. V. ADIGWU & ANOR (2017) LPELR-42763 (SC) PER ONNOGNEN JSC. Therefore, by failing to refute or challenge the affidavits with a counter affidavit, the defendants seriously shot themselves in the foot. The 1st defendant appointed the 2nd defendant as his Special Assistant when she was yet to become a member of the society, this exercise runs contrary to the provisions of our organic law. The LSS Constitution is binding on the Executive, Judiciary, the Legislature and all the members of the Society. We all have the duty to obey its provisions and conduct our activities in a manner that its provisions are not abused or contravened. Any act that is contrary to the provisions of the Constitution is void to the extent of its inconsistency. In this case I have not said that the 1st defendant cannot appoint personal assistants of course he can but he does not have the power to appoint someone outside the Society or someone who is not a member of the Society even a member of the Law Students’ Society of another university is not qualified under our law. I will take the pain to quote from what this court held in the case of OKWARA LAWRENCE & 1 ORS V. THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT LSS & 11 ORS (2021) LCELR 2021001 that: “…What this, therefore, suggests to me is that the 1st defendant has

appointed from amongst the members of the society to assist him as his hand and eyes i.e personal assistance, in the performance of his duties. The word personal according to the Black’s Law Dictionary, 8th edition at p.g1179, defined as “the legal status of one, regarded by law as a person”. On the same token, the word personal assistance is, according to Wikipedia.com, defined as “personal assistant also referred to as personal aide (PA), or personal secretary (PS), is a job title describing a person who assists a person with their daily business or personal tasks.” Also, it is clear from the provision of Section19(6) that, the president can as well delegate his function to any members of the society to represent him on a matter affecting the society, as may appear to him, just and necessary for the progress of the Society.” I will reiterate that the condition precedent for any right or privilege to accrue to any person under the LSS Constitution he or she must be a member of the Society. Where the statutory requirement for doing an act is not complied with any act done in defiance of the law is an exercise in futility. The privilege to be appointed special or personal assistant to the 1st defendant did not come from the blues, it grew out of what is incidental to the power and duty of the 1st defendant under Section 19 (10) the LSS Constitution. See OKWARA LAWRENCE & ANOR. V. THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT OF LSS & 11 OTHERS (SUPRA). Since this is the case, the grant of such a privilege, like the appointment of the 2nd defendant in question, must comply with the Constitution that has donated the power to the 1st defendant which he has failed to do. I am of the opinion that the act of appointing the 2nd defendant as Special Assistant is unconstitutional and the appointment must be declared null and void and it is hereby declarednull and void. From this very moment, the 2nd defendant, Onaja Ufedo, ceases to be a Special Assistant to the Executive President of the Law Students’ Society. This issue is resolved in favour of the Claimant. SALIU WALIYAT AYOMIOTAN, JLSS:  I have had the privilege of reading, in draft, the judgment just delivered by my learned brother, Jamiu Ridwan JLSS, and I entirely agree with his reasoning and conclusion therein .My learned brother has dealt with the issues exhaustively. I must however stress the distinction between rights and privileges under the law, as related to this case. Rights, according to the Black’s Law Dictionary, is defined to mean that which a man is entitled to have, or to do, or to receive from others within the limits prescribed by law. While privilege is referred to be a particular and peculiar benefit or advantage enjoyed by a person, company, or class, beyond the common advantages of other citizens.  

 15 
Thus, this connotes that privilege, as against right, is the enjoyment of a special advantage which most people do not have. In fact, I find the European case of E.B. V. FRANCE ( [2008] ECHR 43546/02 (22 JANUARY 2008) very apposite to this case as the Court judiciously stated that “the difference between a privilege and a right is decisive… Put in the simplest terms, the theoretical principle according to which a right is subject to litigation and according to which a violation of that right requires a remedy does not apply to situations in which a privilege is being granted. An exaggerated example of such a situation would be the privilege of being granted a decoration or a prize, or other situations of special treatment reserved for those who are exceptionally deserving. In other words, it would be “bizarre” for anybody to claim that he ought to have received a particular award, a particular decoration or a particular privilege.”  For the above stated reason and the detailed ones adumbrated in the lead judgment which I hereby adopt, I feel that the appointment of the 2nd Defendant as a special Assistant to the President of the Law Student Society is not valid as the 2nd defendant was not a bona fide member of the society at the point of the appointment, though the appointment was a mere privilege.  MOHAMMAD THANNI ADAM, JLSS: (DISSENTING)  I have had the privilege to read the lead judgment just delivered by my learned brother Jamiu Ridwan, JLSS, he has beautifully dealt with all the issues in contention with proper application of the law. However, as the rule of law will not frown at judicial activism it is my belief that a privilege is a certain entitlement to immunity granted to some people, while a right is an inherent entitlement. The above is not the issue, however, what has made me to think in another way round for justice to be done is that: will it not be an act of shooting the second defendant in the leg by saying that it is only the members of law students society that can be appointed as personal assistants to the 1stdefendant? The provision of section 5 of the LSS Constitution which reads as follows: 

 16 
“The membership of the society shall consist of duly admitted and matriculated students of the Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin.” Reading this provision  in conjunction with section 6 of  the same Constitution only have the effect of clarifying who is a member that can enjoy the right stated in section 7 of the same constitution not who can enjoy privileges and as of today no law in our jurisdiction has actually stated what can be construed as privilege and who can enjoy it to the exclusion of others with the requirements for such an enjoyment being stated. We should try to expand the provision of section 19 (10 ) beyond a simple imagination that the 1st defendant can only exercise such power by appointing only members of the Society , are we saying for the purpose of expediency in his office he cannot appoint any other members of university of Ilorin community has his aid? Such will be fatal.  Society which includes duly admitted student of law, duly admitted students of other faculties in University of Ilorin community such the 2nd defendant can be appointed as personal assistant to The 1st defendant been a member of University of Ilorin community as the law did not state how The 1st defendant will exercise such right granted to him under Section 19 subsection 10 of the LSS Constitution though such should not be without limitations. In my opinion the appointment of the 2nd defendant as Special Assistant is valid.
