RATIO DECENDI  
1. ELECTION: MEANING OF AN ELECTION MATTER  
An election matter in simple terms can be described as a matter which bothers on any 
dispute arising from an election, whether arising before, during or after an election. See 
the case of ADEGBUYI S. OLUFEMI V INEC AND ORS (2008) LPELR-4764 (CA). 
Per- Hon Judge Abdulquadri Abdullah, CJLSS, at Pg. 7 
2. PRACTICE AND PROCEDURE:  MODE OF COMMENCING IN ACTION - 
ELECTION PETITION 
“...it is important at this juncture that I lay emphasis on it that election matters are sui 
generis in nature and for that reason they usually differ from civil or criminal 
proceedings. They are usually brought to court by an election petition. The LSS Court 
Rules is clear on the proper mode of commencing an election matter. Order 2 Rule 3 
provides as follows; 
„ An  action for a claim of irregularity or non -compliance with the law whether 
constitutional or statutory and whether bordering on a pre -election or a post -election 
matter, in elections or electoral procedures, shall be commenced by petitions and the 
Independent Student Electoral Commission shall be joined or deemed as a respondent.‟ 
In light of the above provision of the LSS court rules, election matters, like this one 
before this Honourable Court, are to be commenced by way of an election petition, not 
originating summons.”  Per- Judge Abdulquadri Abdullah CJLSS, at Pg. 8

wordings of a statute are clear enough, ordinary and dictionary meaning shall be adopted.

RULING ON THE DEFENDANT’S PRELIMINARY OBJECTION 
This matter was brought to life before this Honourable Court by way of Originating summons 
filed and dated 13th December 2018, brought by the claimant pursuant to Order 2, Rule 2 of the 
LSS (Civil Procedure Court Rules) wherein the claimant sought the foll owing relief before this 
Honourable Court; 
1. A DECLARATION OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT that the provision of section 30 
of LSSEL is mandatory and not permissive 
2. A DECLARATION OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT that the defendant is mandated 
to maintain order so as to achieve a free, fair, credible and transparent election. 
3.  A DECLARATION OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT that a candidate is not allowed 
by the law to submit his purchased candidature form a day before the election. 
4. A DECLARATION OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT that the defendant failed to 
comply with the provisions of the law which they swore to uphold at all times. 
5. AN ORDER OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT mandating the defendant to write an 
apology letter to all members of the 200 level constituency for failing to comply with the 
provisions of the law in conducting the election into LSS House of Representatives for 200 level 
constituency. 
6. AN ORDER OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT mandating the defendants to comply 
with the provisions of the law within a specified time. 
7. AN ORDER OF THIS HONOURABLE COURT mandating the defendants to pay a sum 
of ten thousand naira to the claimant as to the cost of this litigation. 
8. AND FOR SUCH OTHER ORDERS AND DECLARATIONS THIS COURT MAY 
DEEM FIT IN THE CIRCUMSTANCES OF THIS CASE.  
Attached with the Originating summon is a 15 -paragraph Claimant‟s affidavit in support and a 
written address in support of the originating summons. Couched in the originating summons for 
the just determination of this Honourable Court were two issues.

On issue one, which bothers on whether or not the matter before this Honourable Court is an 
election matter, it is first necessary to determine what an election matter is before we can 
determine whether or not this matter is an election matter. An election matter in simple terms can 
be described as a matter which bothers on any dispute arising from an election, whether arising 
before, during or after an election. See the case of ADEGBUYI S. OLUFEMI V INEC AND 
ORS (2008) LPELR -4764 (CA ). Since an election is a process with a series of stages and 
procedures, election matters can either be a pre -election matter or post -election matter. In the 
recent case of HON. TOBIA CHUCKS EGEONU V INDEPENDENT NATIONAL 
ELECTORAL COMMISSION (Suit no: CA/ OW/103/2013), the Court of Appeal in 
describing and defining pre -election and post -election matters and what constitutes same, Per 
AJI, J.C.A., explains as follows; 
„What then constitutes pre and post -election matters in Nigeria? Olatubora Aderemi, in 
“Electoral Law and Practice in Nigeria” written in honour of Hon. Justice Salihu Modibbo Alfa 
Belgore, 2006, Aderemi Olatubora & Co., Akure, Ondo, p.11 -12, set the parameters for pre -
election matters to encompass preparations towards election that often involv e issues such as 
registration of voters, delineation of constituencies, formation of political parties and regulation 
of the conduct of such political parties for the purpose of selecting members of such parties who 
are to contest for political offices, no mination of candidates for offices and the screening of 
candidates for such offices to ascertain whether such candidates are qualified for the election into 
that office.” While post -election matters are: “complaints against the conduct of the election or 
what happened during the period which the conducts of the election lasted. There are however 
instances when the echoes of unresolved election matters still resonate in legal contests 
questioning the returns in an election…‟ 

8 |  P a g e
 
See also the case of MRS PAULINE MIANAKERE V PEOPLE’S DEMOCRATIC PARTY 
& ORS (Suit No: CA/A/328/2013). 
Thus, to determine whether or not this matter is an election petition, the originating process of 
the claimant shall be examined and the prayers therein will be considered. Prayers three, four and 
five of the claimant as contained in the originating summons clearly shows that the claimant 
prays this Honourable Court; to determine that there was an irregularity or non -compliance with 
the electoral law in the screening of a candidate  in an  election; to mandate the defendants to 
write an apology letter to members of the affected constituency for the irregularity in the 
election; and to mandate the defendants to comply with law by conducting another election 
respectively. In the same vein, paras 8-14 of the claimant‟s affidavit in support of the originating 
summons reveals that the claimant deposes to certain facts which prove the irregularities or non -
compliance with the provisions of the electoral law by the defendant -commission. As such, it  
becomes prima facie clear that this matter brought by the claimant before this Honourable Court 
is an election matter. Thus, issue one is resolved in favour of the defendants and against the 
plaintiff. 
On issue two, which bothers on whether or not the ori ginating summons used by the claimant is 
consistent with the provision of Order 2 Rule 3 of the Law Students‟ Society , it is important at 
this juncture that I lay emphasis on it that election matters are sui generis in nature and for that 
reason they usual ly differ from civil or criminal proceedings. They are usually brought to court 
by an election petition. See OKONKWO V NGIGE (2006) 8 NWLR (PT. 981) 119, AND 
ALL PROGRESSIVE CONGRESS V PEOPLE’S DEMOCRATIC PARTY & ORS (SUIT 
NO CA/EK/EPT/GOV/1/2015) . The pro vision of the LSS Court Rules is clear on the proper 
mode of commencing an election matter. Order 2 Rule 3 provides as follows; 
„An action for a claim of irregularity or non -compliance with the law whether constitutional or 
statutory and whether bordering on a pre -election or a post -election matter, in elections or 
electoral procedures, shall be commenced by petitions and the Independent Student Electoral 
Commission shall be joined or deemed as a respondent.‟  
In light of the above provision of the LSS cour t rules, election matters, like this one before this 
Honourable Court, are to be commenced by way of an election petition, not originating 
summons. Thus, the originating summons used by the claimant in commencing this suit is 

9 |  P a g e
 
inconsistent with Order 2 rule 3 of the LSS Court Rules. As such, issue two is resolved in favour 
of the defendants and against the claimant. 
It is a settled matter of law that the issue of jurisdiction of the court is determined by reliefs 
sought by the claimant in their originating processes and statement of claim. This is as held in the 
case of A.G. ONDO V. MOSES TENE & ORS (2015) LPELR -25730 (CA). It is evident to 
this court that considering the reliefs of the claimant as contained in their originating summons 
that this matter is on e which ought to be brought by a petition as contained in Order 2 rule 3 of 
the LSS Court rules because the matter bothers largely on allegation of non –compliance with a 
provision of the Electoral law and as held in the case of NGIGE V. INEC (2015) 1 NWLR  (PT 
1440) 281 SC, a matter of non -compliance with the provision of the Electoral Act in an election 
is an electoral matter which should be brought by a petition. 
Therefore, considering the provisions of Order 3 rule 1 and the correct position of the law, this 
honourable court hereby finds the preliminary objection of the defendant meritorious. As such 
this honourable court cannot proceed with this case. Hence the preliminary objection is resolved 
in favour of the defendant and the case is struck out.

OPINION OF HON. JUDGE ABDULKAREEM ABDULRAZAQ 
Before now, I was privileged to read in draft the opinion of the learned Chief Judge just 
delivered. I don't have any disparity with the view because it represents the true position of law.  
However, I will add a little bit to the beautiful ruling. I have couched one issue for determination, 
Whether this suit should be instituted by Originating summon or petition? 
The preliminary objection (herein PO) had raised some dust struggling for settlement before this 
Honourable court, this is in term of Origi nating summon and Petition. Originating summon is 
deployed to initiate proceeding where facts are not likely to be in dispute i.e non -contentious 
actions. See order 2 rule 2 of LSS Court (civil procedure) rules 2011 herein called COURT 

10 |  P a g e
 
RULES, INAKOJU V ADE LEKE(2007) 4 NWLR [1023], FASEYE V A. G. OSUN ST. 
(2015) 16 NWLR [1485] 261, DANLADI V DANGIRI (2015) 2 NWLR [1442] 174 SC. 
In the spirit of Order 2 rule 3 of Court Rules that “proceedings may be begun by Originating 
motion or by petition whereby those rules or under any Written Law, the proceedings in question 
are required or authorised to be so begun but not otherwise.” [Emph asis supplied]. Electoral 
matters have been held in cases to be sui generis because they have their specific law that takes 
charge of any matter that arises thereof. See AKPAMGBO-OKADIGBO V. CHIDI (NO. 2) 
(2015) 10 NWLR (1466) 124, APC V. PDP & ORS (2015) LEPLR-25704. The law that 
regulates Election and Electoral cases in our society is THE LAW STUDENTS' SOCIETY 
ELECTORAL LAW 2018 (herein called Electoral Law) which falls within the contemplation of 
written laws so provided for above. 
In Electoral Law, sec. 58(1) states thus “No election and return at an election under this law shall 
be questioned in any manner other than by a PETITION complaining of an undue election or 
return...". [Italics and Emphasis supplied]. Also, sec. 61(1)b states grounds upon which  election 
can be challenged, one of which is non -compliance with the Electoral Law. It is a trite principle 
of law that provision of a statute should not be read in isolation rather holistic interpretation 
should be applied. See SAVANNAH BANK & ANOR V. AJI LO & ANOR (1989) LPELR -
SC 188/1987. The community reading of these provisions means that any question including 
non-compliance against any election in our society shall be initiated by Election petition and not 
otherwise. 
It is the rule of interpretation that when the wordings of a statute are clear enough, ordinary and 
dictionary meaning shall be adopted. SEE LAWAL TOHEEB ABDULRAZAQ V. THE 
ELECTORAL COMMISSION LSS & 2 ORS (SUJ/012/2016) @ 33, THE STUDENTS' 
UNION, UNILORIN V. PRESIDENT, NATIONAL ASS. OF SCIENCE STUDENTS &  6 
ORS (2014) 2USULR . In the same vein, Expressio Unis Est Exclusio Alterius, sec. 58 of 
Electoral Law mentions Electoral Petition which excludes all other modes of commencement of 
an action. See PDP V. INEC (199) 11 NWLR [626] 200, BUHARI V. DIKKO YUSUF (2003) 
14 NWLR [841] 446. 
Notably, claimant Counsel argued that the reliefs sought by claimant is mostly declaratory and 
only wants the court to explain whether a particular provision is mandatory or discret ionary. To 

11 |  P a g e
 
me, this contention does not hold water in this context because what necessitate the interpretation 
should also be considered and the court does not only look at the relieves but all other necessary 
applications before it to decide a case, see COP ABIA ST. V. UZOMBA OKARA & ORS 
(SUIT NO CA/OW/1M/2012.  The fact of this case at hand was one that emanated from 
Electoral exercise; it will be out of hand to argue that it is a matter of mere interpretation because 
Electoral matters are sui generis. 
The careful perusal of claimant affidavit particularly para 8 -14, the fact or set of facts that 
form(s) cause of action in this case, degenerated from an election conducted on 16th day of 
November 2018 which is alleged to be non -compliance. Without doubt, a r easonable person will 
not think twice before concluding that the matter in court is an Electoral matter which implies the 
Electoral Law of our society applies.  
Therefore, the mode deployed to initiate the proceeding is wronged and same is discountenanced 
with. In COP ABIA ST. V. UZOMBA OKARA & ORS SUPRA , it was held that where a 
mode is prescribed for the commencement of an action by a statute or rules of Court, the same 
mode should be religiously adopted. Otherwise, an action wrongly commenced will amount  to a 
breach of due process of law; as such the jurisdiction of the court will be whisk away because the 
matter will be incompetent. If the court should entertain such matter without striking it out, the 
court will only labour in futility. 
Notably, the cou rt has referred to a particular law that is not cited by either of the parties. The 
court does not act out of its whims and caprices but rather in line with the legal position. Sec. 
122 of Evidence Act 2011 has established that court should take Judicial N otice of any 
Legislation validly made by the legislature. Also, in the case of KABO AIR LIMITED V. THE 
O' CORPORATION LIMITED (2014) AELR 4773, it was held  that one of the instances to 
raise issue Suo Motu and decide it Suo Motu is when parties have ignored, refused or did not cite 
a statute that has bearing force on the case before it. Therefore, parties‟ right of fair hearing is not 
affected at all.
