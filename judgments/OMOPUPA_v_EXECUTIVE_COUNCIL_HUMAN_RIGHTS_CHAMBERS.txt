2. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW- WHETHER CONSTITUTIONAL ISSUES CAN BECOME 
ACADEMIC 
“This Honourable Court ruled that no court of law will knowingly act in vain, and the general attitude of 
the court is that it always loathes making pronouncements on an academic or hypothetical issue as it does 
not serve any purpose. The court further held that there is no doubt that in the absence of any live issue in 
a suit, it becomes extinct and any activity on it will be a mere academic exercise. However, based on the 
fact that the constitution is a living and organic document that always run and operate, constitutional 
matters always have life in them and cannot become academic in nature. As such, constitutional matters 
can be described as an exception to academ ic matters. The court in its ruling relied on the case of 
KALEJAIYE MUSA V. HUMAN RIGHT CHAMBER LSSJ/004/2018, AG ABIA v AG FEDERATION 
(2003) 4 NWLR (pt. 809) 124, MILITARY GOVERNOR OF ONDO STATE v ADEWUNMI (1988) 3 
NWLR (PT. 82) 280 and NWAGWE v OKERE (20 08) FWLR (PT 431) p.843 ”  PER ABDULLAHI 
(CJLSS) at Pg. 6 
3. INTERPRETATION- ON THE PURPORT OF ARTICLE 13 OF THE CONSTITUTION OF 
HUMAN RIGHTS CHAMBERS 2018 
I am of the firm view that the use of the word „shall‟ Article 13 General Meeting (1) of the Constitution 
of Human Rights Chambers 2018 imports mandatoriness and the provision must be complied with fully. I 
hereby resolve the first issue in favour of the claimant PER ABDULLAHI (CJLSS) at Pg. 7 
4. LOCUS STANDI- ON WHO HAS LOCUS STANDI TO SUE LSS CHAMBERS EXECUTIVES 
The court also ruled that Claimant has locus standi in this case because he is member of the said 
chambers who is governed by the constitution of the said chambers and that the provision to be construed 
is contained in that constitution. He needs nothing m ore to be conferred appropriate Locus standi. See 
SOLIU AFEEZ ISHOLA V THE SENATE PRESIDENT & ORS (SUJ/002/18 -19), FAWEHINMI V. 
PRESIDENT, FRN (2007) 14 NWLR [1054] 275” per Abdulquadir Abdullahi, CJLSS at Pg. 7 
5. WORDS AND PHRASES- SHALL 
The word “shall” when used in a statutory provision import anything must be done, it is a form of 
command or mandate, the use and meaning of the word “shall” in a statute has been the   subject of 
judicial attention in number of cases of this court and appellate court (student union court) in all those 
decisions ,the courts have emphasized that in its ordinary meaning “shall” is a word of command which 
must be given an obligatory meanin g as denoting compulsion and has the invaluable consequence of 
excluding the thought of discretion of imposing a duty which must be enforced. I rely on the cases of 
OMOPUPA AWWAL V P.R.O. 1 HUMAN RI GHT CHAMBERS Suit No: LSSJ/001/2019 and 
SOLIU HAFEEZ ISHOL A V THE SENATE PRESIDENT OF THE STUDENT UNION & ORS 
SUJ/OU2/18-19 PER ABDULLAHI (CJLSS) at Pg.7

defendant is the Executive C ouncil of the Human Rights chambers. The defendant didn‟t organize a weekly

Attached with the originating summons is a 14 paragraph affidavit deposed to by one ELEJA HUSSEIN

On 28 th of January, 2019 the date fixed for hearing, M. A. Omopupa, the c laimant representing himself, 
adopted his brief as filed before this Honourable Court. In the claimant‟s originating summons, two issues were 
distilled for determination by this Honourable Court which is as follows; 
1. Whether or not the use of the word “shal l” as contained in the provisions of Article 13 General Meeting

2. Whether or not the defendant in this instant case has breached the provisions of the Articl e 13 General

In his written address, the claimant submitted, on issue one, that the word “shall” in article 13 General 
Meeting (1) of the constitution of Human Right chambers, 2018, is to be construed as mandating and the rules

denotes obligations and this gives no discre tion to the defendants relying on the case ALADETAN V.  
OGUNYEM & ORS (2010) LPELR 3699 C.A, KALAMU V GUNRIM (2003) 16 N.W.L.R (PT 847) P.517 
AND INEC & ORS V INAMA & ORS (2007) LPELR 8063. The claimant argued further that the provisions in 
statutes must be given their simple and direct meaning and the court is not to go outside the words of the statute

in search of an interpretation which is convenient to the court or parties, even when the provision of the statute is 
hard. He relied on LAWAL TOYEEB ABDULR ASSAQ V. THE ELECTORAL COMMISSION, LAW

On issue two, it was su bmitted by the claimant that the defendant has breached the sacred provisions of

In response to the claimant‟s originating summons, the defendants filed a preliminary objection dated 20 th

ii. That the claimant had no locus standi. 
In the Preliminary objection, the defendants made the following prayers before this Honourable Court:

A sole issue was couched by the defendants in the preliminary objection which is as follows;

2. That the claimant has no locus standi. 
3. That the claimant has no reasonable cause of action. 
4. That the case of the claimant is outside the jurisprudential competence of this Honourable Court.

On the first standpoint, the defendant contended that the suit in question is academic in nature as it does not 
apply to any practical situation of human nature or hu manity and is of no utilitarian value to the claimant even if 
judgment is given in his favour. The defendants further argued that the court would not waste its precious time on 
issues that no longer have life and that since the claimant has not shown in hi s affidavit in support of the 
originating summons any material fact that could convince the court that where judgment is given in his favour, it 
will be of any value to him. They submitted on the standpoint that suits that are academic and speculative in court 
are not to be entertained or adjudicated on them, not guesses relying on the case of KAWUWA & ANOR v,

On the second standpoint, the defendant contended that the claimant has no locus standi or the capacity to 
institute this suit before this Honourable Court and as such has no right to be heard. The defendant further 
contended that the claimant's affidavit showed that he has no sufficient or tangible interest in this case because he 
has failed to show any. The defendants submitted on this standpoint that a claimant has to show and satisfy the 
court that he has sufficient legal interest that was violated in the subject matter of the action. The defendants cited

action and as such lacks the right to sue. 
On the fourth standpoint, the defendant submitted relying on the arguments and averments in the earlier

In an oral reply given by the claimant‟s counsel, A. A. Mustapha, on the 28th of January, 2019 to the 
preliminary objection of the defendant, the claimant counsel adopted  the issues as distilled by the defendants in

On the first issue, the claimant‟s counsel argued and submitted that this matter is not academic in nature as it

On the second iss ue, the claimant‟s counsel argued and submitted that the claimant has locus standi to 
institute the action. The counsel further argued that it is trite in law that in constitutional matters, every member 
of the society to which the constitution is applicab le has the legal standing or locus standi to institute an action 
against any authority which has failed to carry out its constitutional duty because barring members of the society 
from instituting an action to uphold constitutional matters can lead to a gr oss violation of the constitution. The 
counsel strengthened his position with the case of SOLIU AFEEZ ISHOLA V THE SENATE PRESIDENT &

On the third standpoint, the claimant‟s counsel argued and submitted that a careful perusal of the c laimant‟s 
affidavit, particularly paras 6 -8 reveals glaringly facts that show that the claimant has a cause of action. He

On the fourth standpoint and on the whole, the claimant's counsel submitted that this Honourable Court has

the preliminary objection, this Honourable Court determined the preliminary object in favour of the claimant and 
dismissed the preliminary objection and ruled that it has the requisite jurisdiction to hear the matter.

RIGHT CHAMBER LSSJ/004/2018, AG ABIA v AG FED ERATION (2003) 4 NWLR (pt. 809) 124, 
MILITARY GOVERNOR OF ONDO STATE v ADEWUNMI (1988) 3 NWLR (PT. 82) 280 and NWAGWE v 
OKERE (2008) FWLR (PT 431) p.843 . The court also ruled that the cause of action in a matter begun by 
Originating Summons can be determin ed from the claimant’s affidavit relying on the authorities of

On the second issue, it is evidence before the court that the school is on New Year break on the bulk of 
the first week of January and resumed on Thursday, 3 rd of January, 2019. The defendant failed to hold General 
meeting on the second week of January for reason best known to them. It is also evidenced before the Court that 
the defendant held a general meeting on 15 th of January, 2019. If the provision of Article 13 on General Meeting 
(1) of the Constit ution of Human Rights Chambers 2018 provides for the general meeting to be held once in 
every two weeks in a situation whereby it is not practicable to hold it every week (the determination of it 
impracticability which is at the discretion of executive cou ncil), A mathematical calculation which will aid the 
just determination of this case is whether it is up to two weeks after resumption before the general meeting was 
held. If the school resumes on 3 rd of January, two weeks after will lapse at 17 th of January within which a general 
meeting was held. 
There is no averment in the claimant affidavit that points to an unconstitutional act of the defendant thus 
by adopting the literal rule of interpretation at the face of an unambiguous provision of the apposite l aws, the 
defendant acted in tandem with the provision of the constitution. I placed heavy reliance on the cases of A.G 
OGUN STATE V A.G FEDERATION [2002] 18 NWLR [PT.798] p232 and ZANFARA STATE HOUSE 
OF ASSEMBLY SERVICE COMMISSION & ORS V GOVERNOR OF ZANFA RA STATE & ORS
