EQUITABLE MAXIM: UBI JUS IBI REMEDIUM The equitable maxim that ubi jus ibi remedium which means there is a right there must be a remedy. The maxim does not operate as a matter of course; it operates with another equitable maxim that equity follows the law. This is to say where no legal right is established by a party seeking a remedy; equity will not come to his aid. See ASHBY V. WHITE (1703) 92 ER 126.  2. INTERPRETATION OF STATUTE: LITERAL RULE OF INTERPRETATION The law is trite that where the law court is faced with the interpretation of a statute especially the constitution the literal rule of interpretation must be used by giving the words in the statute their grammatical meanings. In the case of ABDULLAHI DAGBO V. FINANCIAL SECRETARY, HUMAN RIGHT CHAMBERS (2019) LCELR 1819003, per Ketu, CJLSS (as he then was) said in very beautiful terms that it is trite law that whenever the position of the law is clear and unambiguous, courts should not become fashion designers to shape and design new cloth for the statute. This means that statutes should not be given another cloth so far its original cloth does not portray irrationality 

 3 
and injustice. See ELELU-HABEEB V A.G FEDERATION AND ORS (2012) 1 NWLR [1318] 423 SC, DASUKI AHMAD OLUWNISHOLA & ANOR V. HUMAN RIGHTS CHAMBERS & ORS (SUIT NO. LSSJ/005/2018).The court may do otherwise where reliance on the literal rule will lead to an absurdity.  3. LAW OF CONTRACT: CONSIDERATION - WHETHER PERFORMANCE OF A LEGAL DUTY CONSTITUTES CONSIDERATION  The law is trite that a legal duty does not suffice as consideration for a promise. See COLLINS V. GODEFROY 1831 1 B & AD 950. 4. LAW OF CONTRACT: CONSIDERATION – WHETHER PERFORMANCE OF A PRE-EXISTING OBLIGATION CONSTITUTES CONSIDERATION The law is settled that where a party performs an act which is merely a discharge of a pre-existing obligation, there is no consideration for a promise made to him.  5. LAW OF CONTRACT: DEFINITION OF CONTRACT   A contract is an agreement between two or more parties creating obligations that are enforceable or otherwise recognizable in law. See the case of ROBINET NIGERIA LTD V. SHELL NIG. GAS LTD (2013) LPELP-22144 (CA). It is the basic principle of law that whenever there is an exchange of promises which has led to an agreement between persons for the performance of a certain activity, the strong pillars of valid contract such as offer, acceptance and consideration will have been established in that case. And when the agreement has attained the status of a valid contract, should there be any breach of duty; the aggrieved person can bring an action for a redress before any court of law with requisite jurisdiction. See ISSA HABEEBULLAHI V. AKANNI (2021) LCELR 2021003; ALADE V. ALIC (2010) 19 NWLR PT [1226] 111. 6. LAW OF CONTRACT: DEFINITION OF AN OFFER  An offer is a statement or other indication that an individual is prepared to enter into a contract which must be clear, unequivocal and expressed in a manner capable of acceptance. See MR PETER YUSEFUL ONIMIYA V. ACCESS BANK PLC (2014) AELR 3160 (CA). 7. Fact of the case  The plaintiff/claimant is a member of the Human Rights Chambers of Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin. The defendants are also Executive members of the same Chambers. On the 11th July 2021, the defendants notified members of Chambers of its weekly intellectual forum. There is no dispute on whether the claimant is a member of the Chambers; in fact he is a dutiful and responsible Activist. He attends the Chambers meetings like any responsible member of any club or association will do. Why I said the Claimant is a responsible member of Chambers will be clarified later in this judgment. Claimant attended the meeting of the Chambers held on the 13th July 2021 as usual. He has a very enduring track record in this regard. These facts can be gleaned from paragraph 1, 2, and 6 of claimant‟s statement of claim and same was admitted in paragraph 2 and paragraph 6 of the PW1‟s evidence-in-chief. Claimant alleged that no bus came immediately after the forum as promised by the defendants.  Being aggrieved by what he considered to be a breach of contract on the part of the defendants, he approached this honourable court to claim remedy against the defendants. The claimant prayed for the following reliefs;

 RESOLUTION  Before I delve into the business of determining the question of to which side the imaginary scale of justice tilts in this case, the party that is wrong and that which is right, I find the issues couched by the parties to be clumsy. For the sake of saving the precious and splendid time of this court, I have couched a sole issue for the proper determination of this case which is;  WHETHER OR NOT, CONSIDERING THE CIRCUMSTANCES SURROUNDING  THIS CASE, THERE IS A VALID AGREEMENT BETWEEN THE PARTIES THAT IS  CAPABLE OF ENFORCEMENT  A contract is an agreement between two or more parties creating obligations that are enforceable or otherwise recognizable in law. See the case of ROBINET NIGERIA LTD V. SHELL NIG. GAS LTD (2013) LPELP-22144 (CA). It is the basic principle of law that whenever there is an exchange of promises which has led to an agreement between persons for the performance of a certain activity, the strong pillars of valid contract such as offer, acceptance and consideration will have been established in that case. And when the agreement has attained the status of a valid contract, should there be any breach of duty; the aggrieved person can bring an action for a redress before any court of law with requisite jurisdiction. See ISSA HABEEBULLAHI V. AKANNI (2021) LCELR 2021003; ALADE V. ALIC (2010) 19 NWLR PT [1226] 111  At this juncture I will not hesitate to embark on a voyage of discovery to see if there was any indication of an offer in the statement made by the defendants. An offer is a statement or other indication that an individual is prepared to enter into a contract which must be clear, unequivocal and expressed in a manner capable of acceptance. See MR PETER YUSEFUL ONIMIYA V. ACCESS BANK PLC (2014) AELR 3160 (CA). Where the existence of a valid offer cannot be established in an agreement the other elements of a contract will be displaced. This is to say that a discussion on acceptance or consideration cannot reasonably arise where there is no offer which is the gateway to the other elements of a valid contract. Claimant is a member of the Human Rights Chambers and by the virtue of being a member; he is subject to the Human Rights Chambers Constitution. I find the provision of Article 5 Section 6 Subsection 5 of the Human 

 11 
Rights Chambers Constitution to be indispensable in dealing with the contention of the claimant. The provision of the organic law of the Chambers says; “A member shall attend all meetings; participate in discussions, debates and deliberations” „A Member‟ is this provision is not referring to any persons; „A member‟ is a member of the Human Right Chambers. Claimant is a member of the Chambers. Claimant urged this court to interpret the word “shall” to mean permissive and not obligatory. What does the law say? Is shall performing the function of May in this provision? Should I succumb to the urge of the claimant? I do not think so. The law is trite that where the law court is faced with the interpretation of a statute especially the constitution the literal rule of interpretation must be used by giving the words in the statute their grammatical meanings. In the case of ABDULLAHI DAGBO V. FINANCIAL SECRETARY, HUMAN RIGHT CHAMBERS (2019) LCELR 1819003, per Ketu, CJLSS (as he then was) said in very beautiful terms that it is trite law that whenever the position of the law is clear and unambiguous, courts should not become fashion designers to shape and design new cloth for the statute. This means that statutes should not be given another cloth so far its original cloth does not portray irrationality and injustice. See ELELU-HABEEB V A.G FEDERATION AND ORS (2012) 1 NWLR [1318] 423 SC, DASUKI AHMAD OLUWNISHOLA & ANOR V. HUMAN RIGHTS CHAMBERS & ORS (SUIT NO. LSSJ/005/2018). The court may do otherwise where reliance on the literal rule will lead to an absurdity. I do not agree with the claimant that “shall” employed in Article 5 gives the members of the chambers the choice to choose meetings they should attend and which they should not. It is trite law that when “shall” is used it usually connotes an obligation or it imposes a duty. See TAOFIK WALIU V. THE CLERK LSS HOUSE OF REPRESENTATIVES (2019) LCELR 1819001. I am of the view that every member of the Human Rights Chambers has a duty imposed on him or her to attend the meetings of the Chambers. It is an attribute of a responsible member of the Chambers. This is significant for the objectives of the Chambers to be achieved. The objectives cannot be achieved by addressing tables and chairs at the meetings or by taking the forums of the chambers to individual houses of the members. God forbid that day. Members of the Human Right Chambers owe the chamber a duty to be responsible and this responsibility can be displayed by being an active Activist of the Chambers. It is clear to my mind like the moon of the fourteenth day that the Chambers Constitution imposes a duty on every member of Human Rights Chambers to attend its meetings and also participate in the discussions organized by the Chambers. And I so hold. I do not have 

 12 
any difficulty in agreeing with counsel to the defendants, I.O Kolawole that the claimant having submitted to the Human Rights Chambers Constitution cannot come to court and complain on his experiences in discharging the duty. The law is settled that where a party performs an act which is merely a discharge of a pre-existing obligation, there is no consideration for a promise made to him. Claimant attended the meeting in pursuance of the duty imposed on him by the Chambers Constitution and for him to use the discharge of such statutory duty as an artifice to claim compensation is in my opinion unreasonable, such a claim is untenable. The claim of the claimant is what will make a Hausa man on the street of Kano to exclaim Haba! In order to drive my point home, I will make a reference to the evidence of the two witnesses called by the claimant. Under cross-examination PW1 testified that: “Claimant is my classmate and we are close to some extent. He attends his Chambers meetings from time to time because he is a responsible member of his Chambers” PW2 also testified: “I am a member of the Human Right Chambers. As responsible member I attend the Chambers‟ meetings every week. Claimant attends the meetings also because he is a responsible member of the Chambers” The probative value of these statements is weighty. Evidence of a witness against a party who has called him is admissible and is weighty. See LERIC NIG. LTD V. U.B.N. LTD (2000) 12 SCNJ 184. The statements are germane to prove or disprove the issue of whether Exhibit 1 tendered in evidence was an indication of a unilateral contract to the claimant. I agree with counsel to the defendants that claimant attended the Human Right Chambers meeting on the 13th of July 2021 not because he wanted to fulfill an obligation under any purported contract but he attended because he is a dutiful and responsible member of the Chambers. He is aware of his duty to attend Chambers meetings imposed on him under Article 5 of the Human Rights Chambers Constitution; he follows the law because he is responsible. He has a good track record as far as attending meetings is concerned. This is to say that claimant did not attend the meeting on 13th because a bus was promised to him; he attended because he considered it to be an obligation. I am repeating myself, I am not doing it inadvertently I have the intention to create an emphasis. I have carefully evaluated Exhibit 1, it does not qualify or constitute an offer that is capable of being accepted. Exh. 1 did not evince an intention to create any legal relation with members of the Chambers as far as their weekly meeting on the 13th of July was concerned. It is just a mere promise calculated to excite and encourage members of the chambers to attend the meeting. To persuade the responsible members such as the claimant to continue to be responsible and 

 13 
encourage those that are not active members of the Chambers to be dutiful just like the claimants. An informal gratuitous promise without any intention to create legal relation does not amount to contract. If we assume that Exhibit 1 constituted a valid offer and as the Claimant argued that his act of attending the meeting constituted consideration, the claimant as earlier stated has a legal duty he owes the Chambers and the law is trite that a legal duty does not suffice as consideration for a promise. See COLLINS V. GODEFROY 1831 1 B & AD 950 I am aware of the equitable maxim that ubi jus ibi remedium which means where there is a wrong there must be a remedy. The maxim does not operate as a matter of course; it operates with another equitable maxim that equity follows the law. This is to say where no legal right is established by a party seeking a remedy; equity will not come to his aid. See ASHBY V. WHITE (1703) 92 ER 126. If at all equity will be reluctant to follow the law in some cases it is not in the circumstance of this case. Equity is no Father Christmas, even if it is one there is a limit to want it can give. If the claimant has a right, he must have a means to vindicate and maintain it. The claimant does not have any right against the defendants as far as this case is concerned; he has nothing to vindicate, he has nothing to maintain and he is not entitled to any remedy. This court is an honourable court; it is a court of justice and not a court of emotion. I hereby come to the conclusion that there was not valid contract capable of enforcement between the claimant and the defendants; the case of the claimant is hereby dismissed. Parties should bear their respective expenses; I make no order as to cost.
