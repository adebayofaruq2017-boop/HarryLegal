 RATIO DECIDENDI 1. ACTION: CONDITION PRECEDENT- THE MEANING THEREOF  Condition Precedent is an action that must be done or fulfilled before a right can be enjoyed. Take as an example, for a farmer to harvest a crop, he must first plant. Planting of the seeds here is a condition precedent. Legally speaking, where a law provides for a procedure for doing an act, that procedure must be followed for the subsequent act to be valid. Failure to comply with Condition Precedent in a case can go as far as the root of the case thereby affecting the court’s jurisdiction of hearing a case. Another example is that Pre-Action Notice is a Condition Precedent to filing a suit, failure to do this puts the jurisdiction of the court on hold pending the compliance with the condition. See EDE V ACCESS BANK PLC (2020) 4NWLR pt. 1715 pg 417 CA  2. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: LSS COSNTITUTIONAL LAW – POWER OF THE LSS PRESIDENT Our law is trite that the 1st defendant is imbued with the constitutional vire to make this appointment and any other appointments incidental to the exercise of his executive powers. See: OKWARA LAWRENCE V. THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT, LSS & 11 ORS (2021) LCELR 2021001 3. CONSTITUTIONAL LAW: LSS COSNTITUTION – RELEVANCE OF APPOINTMENTS IN COMPLAINCE WITH THE LSS CONSTITUTION The President derives the powers on which he is relying to make this appointment from the constitution itself and as provided for under Section 1 of the LSS Constitution, the constitution is supreme and binding on every member and body within the society. Any act done in violation of the supreme legal document shall be prima facie declared null and void to the extent of its inconsistency. This is just as was held by this court in the case of AKANNI & 2 ORS V THE WELFARE DIRECTOR OF LSS & 2 ORS. See also the case of TIMOTHY V OFORKA (2008) 9 NWLR (Pt. 1091) 204.The provision of Section 19 of the LSS constitution cannot be divorced from the provisions of Sections 5 and 6 of the LSS constitution and cannot be exercised in isolation of same. Thus, the appointments made by the president of the society in exercise of his powers under Section 19 (10) of the constitution must be made in accordance to the provisions of the constitution and specifically Sections 5 and 6 in relation to this case. 4. DOCTRINE OF JUDICIAL PRECEDENT AND STARE DECISIS: INSTANCES WHERE THE COURT WILL DEPART FROM ITS PREVIOUS DECISION            The law is settled that in taking a decision whether or not to depart from its earlier   decision, the court is faced with two competing situations, viz: a. The situation where the stability of the law is in vindication of the doctrine of stare                                                                                                                                                                          decisis is a desideratum: b.  The situation where rigid adherence to the doctrine of stare decisis will ruin or impede the development of the law and the amicable administration of justice  In order to sway to the position of departure, the court must be convinced that taking the      position in (b) above will meet the end of justice and the development of the law. Where there is any doubt as to the reasonability or desirability to follow (b) above, the doubt must be exercised in favour of (a). See: AWUSE V. ODILI (2003) 18 NWLR (PT 851)179 in this instant case. 6. INTERPRETATION OF STATUTE: LITERAL RULE OF INTERPRETATION  The law is trite that in the interpretation of the constitution, the court should prefer such interpretation as would serve the interest and best carry out its object and purpose. Its relevant provisions must be read together and not disjointly and where the words of any section are clear and unambiguous, they must be given their ordinary meaning unless they would lead to absurdity or be in conflict with other provisions of the constitution. See: TINUBU V. I.M.B SECURITIES PLC (2001) 16 NWLR (PT 740) 670 AT 720; IFEZUE V. MBADUGHA (1984) 1 SCNLR 427. The primary function of the court is to search for the intention of the lawmaker in the interpretation of a statute. Where a statue is clear and unambiguous, the court in the exercise of the interpretative jurisdiction must not stop where the statute stops. In order words, a court of law has no jurisdiction to rewrite a statute to suit the purpose of a party or both parties. The moment a court of law intends to rewrite a statute or really rewrites a statute, the intention of the lawmaker is 

4 | Page  
thrown overboard and the court changes places with the lawmaker. In view of the fact that that will be against the doctrine of separation of powers entrenched in the Constitution, a court of law will not embark on such an unconstitutional act. See: ARAKA V. EGBUE (2003) 17 NWLR (PT 848) 1 7. INTERPRETATION OF STATUTE: WHEN ‘AND’ OR ‘OR’ IS USED IN A STATUTE (THE PURPORT THEREOF)  The provision used the word “and” which the court is ordinarily expected to construe as conjunctive. In ordinary usage, the word “or” is disjunctive and the word “and” is conjunctive. However, there are situations which make it necessary to read “and” in the place of “or” and vice versa. This may occur to carry out the intention of the legislature. Such interpretation may be quite useful in order to avoid absurd or impracticable results. Short of such circumstances, the words “or” and “and” being unambiguous words, should be given their ordinary plain meanings notwithstanding the fact that such interpretation will lead to an unreasonable or unfair result. See: NDOMA-EGBA V. CHUKWUOGOR (2004) 6 NWLR (PT. 869) 382 AT 409; AFRICAN NEWSPAPERS LTD V. FEDERAL REPUBLIC OF NIGERIA (1985) 2 NWLR (PT. 6) 137; EGBE V. ALHAJI (1990) 1 NWLR (PT. 128) 546. RESOLUTION  For the purpose of properly dealing with the issues in contention, I have couched a sole issue which I believe will suffice to address the numerous issues that were raised by the parties.  The issue is: WHETHER OR NOT THE APPOINTMENT OF THE 2ND DEFENDANT BY THE 1ST DEFENDANT IS CONSITUTIONAL? Let me firstly reproduce here the following provisions of the LSS Constitution as they are consequential to determining this case: Section 5 “The membership of the society shall consist of duly admitted and matriculated students of the Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin.” Section 6 “MEMBERS (bonafide in respect of the Society) means any member who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society.” The question of who has the right to claim membership of the society has been laid to rest in a long line of cases decided by this court. Before returning to this issue later, let me quickly reiterate the position of this court on the status of the appointment of the 2nd defendant by the 1st defendant. In my view, I agree with the argument of the Defendants that the appointment is not a right and that the appointment does not fall within the auspices of section 7 of the LSS Constitution. Yes, I agree with them! On the other hand, I maintain the view that the appointment of the 2nd defendant as member of Career Advancement Committee is nothing short of a privilege. I will return to this point on privilege later. Our law is trite that the 1st defendant is imbued with the constitutional vire to make this appointment and any other appointments incidental to the exercise of his executive powers. See: OKWARA LAWRENCE V. THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT, LSS & 11 ORS (2021) LCELR 2021001. The consequential question begging for an answer is whether the 2nd defendant should have been appointed considering her student status at the time of appointment. I will answer the question by first answering who a “member” of the society is. The LSS Constitution provides under section 5 that “membership of the society shall consist of duly admitted and matriculated students of Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin.” (Emphasis is mine) By paragraphs 4, 5 and 8 of the affidavits in support of the Claimants’ originating summons, deposed to by the claimants, at the time of filing this suit the 2nd 

9 | Page  
defendant was yet to be matriculated at the time of her appointment. By paragraphs 2 and 6 of the said affidavits, academic activities for fresher commenced on the 15 of December 2022 while the 1st defendant announced the appointment of the 2nd defendant on the 31st of December 2022. Considering the manner in which the appointment was made, can it be said to be valid by the community reading of sections 5 and 6 of the LSS Constitution? Let me refer to what I said earlier that the appointment of the 2nd defendant is just a mere privilege and not a right. The LSS Constitution expressly provides that it is only members of the society who are entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the society. Counsel to defendants argued that even though not yet matriculated, the 2nd defendant should be considered a member of the society since she was already admitted relying on the equitable maxim that equity considers as done that which ought to be done. The LSS Constitution says a member is “a person who is duly admitted and matriculated….” The provision used the word “and” which the court is ordinarily expected to construe as conjunctive. In ordinary usage, the word “or” is disjunctive and the word “and” is conjunctive. However, there are situations which make it necessary to read “and” in the place of “or” and vice versa. This may occur to carry out the intention of the legislature. Such interpretation may be quite useful in order to avoid absurd or impracticable results. Short of such circumstances, the words “or” and “and” being unambiguous words, should be given their ordinary plain meanings notwithstanding the fact that such interpretation will lead to an unreasonable or unfair result. See: NDOMA-EGBA V. CHUKWUOGOR (2004) 6 NWLR (PT. 869) 382 AT 409; AFRICAN NEWSPAPERS LTD V. FEDERAL REPUBLIC OF NIGERIA (1985) 2 NWLR (PT. 6) 137; EGBE V. ALHAJI (1990) 1 NWLR (PT. 128) 546. In this instant case, I ask myself if it is in any way reasonable to interpret “and” in section 5 to mean “or”? I do not think so. This Honourable Court had a similar reasoning in the case of TAOFIK WALIU OPEYEMI V. THE ELECTORAL COMMISSION, LSS & OTHERS LSSJ/005/19 where per Ibidunmi JLSS (as he then was) held that “by the express mentioning of both admission and matriculation in the provision by the draftsmen surely has the purpose it is supposed to serve. The matriculation oath of the University is customarily subscribed to on the day of matriculation. It therefore may have been the intention of the lawmaker that while, by admission, the persons to which this section relates may have been students of the University, they shall not be members of the Society without the operation of “and matriculated” which means that they must both be admitted and must have subscribed to the oath of matriculation usually administered on that day.” One can say that the basis for the strict condition of admission and matriculation in section 7 is that the drafters of the LSS Constitution believed there could occur a situation where a student would be offered admission to Faculty of Law but would not scale through the process of matriculation or a situation where a student would be admitted to read Law but would be moved to another department before matriculation. In view of this, looking again at the argument urged by counsel to the defendants that this court should validate the appointment of the 2nd defendant under the maxim of equity considers as done that which ought to be done, with due respect to them, I will say the argument was misconceived and very heavy to fly. The relevant provisions of the LSS Constitution are too unambiguous than to allow

such a maxim to override the apparent intendment of the drafters and the underlying purpose of the provisions. The law is trite that in the interpretation of the constitution, the court should prefer such interpretation as would serve the interest and best carry out its object and purpose. Its relevant provisions must be read together and not disjointly and where the words of any section are clear and unambiguous, they must be given their ordinary meaning unless they would lead to absurdity or be in conflict with other provisions of the constitution. See: TINUBU V. I.M.B SECURITIES PLC (2001) 16 NWLR (PT 740) 670 AT 720; IFEZUE V. MBADUGHA (1984) 1 SCNLR 427. The primary function of the court is to search for the intention of the lawmaker in the interpretation of a statute. Where a statue is clear and unambiguous, the court in the exercise of the interpretative jurisdiction must not stop where the statute stops. In order words, a court of law has no jurisdiction to rewrite a statute to suit the purpose of a party or both parties. The moment a court of law intends to rewrite a statute or really rewrites a statute, the intention of the lawmaker is thrown overboard and the court changes places with the lawmaker. In view of the fact that that will be against the doctrine of separation of powers entrenched in the Constitution, a court of law will not embark on such an unconstitutional act. See: ARAKA V. EGBUE (2003) 17 NWLR (PT 848) 1. This court has on several occasions given notable decisions on the controversy surrounding who a member of the society is and it has often being reiterated that a person constitutionally becomes a member by not only being admitted but also being duly matriculated; admission alone does not suffice. See: TAOFIK WALIU OPEYEMI V. THE ELECTORSL COMMISSION, LSS & OTHERS LSSJ/005/19; AHMED HABEEB OLAYINKA V. LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT AND 1 ANOR. LSSJ/009/21. I see no cogent ground why I should depart from these decisions. The law is settled that in taking a decision whether or not to depart from its earlier decision, the court is faced with two competing situations, viz: a. The situation where the stability of the law is in vindication of the doctrine of stare decisis is a desideratum: and b. The situation where rigid adherence to the doctrine of stare decisis will ruin or impede the development of the law and the amicable administration of justice. In order to sway to the position of departure, the court must be convinced that taking the position in (b) above will meet the end of justice and the development of the law. Where there is any doubt as to the reasonability or desirability to follow (b) above, the doubt must be exercised in favour of (a). See: AWUSE V. ODILI (2003) 18 NWLR (PT 851)179. In this instant case, I seems to me that it will be unreasonable to depart from the earlier decisions of this court similar to this instant issue. To conclude, I see the appointment of the 2nd defendant as member of the Career Advancement Committee as a privilege given to her out of the executive powers imbued in the 1st defendant and for any student of Faculty of Law, university of Ilorin, to enjoy this privilege he or she must not only be admitted but must be duly matriculated. Since the 1st defendant failed to wait for the appropriate time to make the appointment, the action was premature and failed to meet the constitutional requirements for a valid appointment. RESOLUTION OF ISSUES  In order to lay this case to rest, this court would adopt the following issues for the just determination of this case.  1. WHETHER OR NOT SECTION 5 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION IS A CONDITION PRECEDENT TO SECTION 7 OF THE SAME CONSTITUTION.  The keyword in this issue is Condition Precedent. The Supreme Court of Nigeria in the recent case of UNIVERSAL PROPERTY LIMITED V PINNACLE COMMUNICATIONS BANK (2022) 12 NWLR (PT. 1845) 523 SC, defined condition precedent in the following words: “Condition Precedent is a provision which makes the existence of a right dependent on the happening of an event; the right is then Conditional as opposed to an absolute right..... A Condition Precedent is one that delays the vesting of a right until the happening of an event.” (Emphasis mine). See also the case of AG KWARA V ADEYEMO (2017) 1 NWLR (Pt. 1546) 210 SC as well as the case of FRN v NGANJIWA (2022) 17 NWLR (Pt. 1860) 407 SC. Simply put, Condition Precedent is an action that must be done or fulfilled before a right can be enjoyed. Take as an example, for a farmer to harvest a crop, he must first plant. Planting of the seeds here is a condition precedent. Legally speaking, where a law provides for a procedure for doing an act, that procedure must be followed for the subsequent act to be valid. Failure to comply with Condition Precedent in a case can go as far as the root of the case thereby affecting the court’s jurisdiction of hearing a case. Another example is that Pre-Action Notice is a Condition Precedent to filing a suit, failure to do this puts the jurisdiction of the court on hold pending the compliance with the condition. See EDE V ACCESS BANK PLC (2020) 4NWLR pt. 1715 pg 417 CA Coming down to our own jurisdiction here, is SECTION 5 of the LSS CONSTITUTION really a condition precedent to SECTION 7 of the same constitution? It’s best to reproduce the provisions of these Sections for clarity and lucidity. SECTION 5 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION “The membership of the society shall consist of duly admitted and matriculated students of the Faculty of Law, University of Ilorin.” SECTION 7 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION (1) Every member shall have the right to vote and be voted for, without prejudice to any of the provisions of this Constitution. (2) Every member shall have the right to attend all programmes organized by the Society, including seminars, symposia, public lectures, moot and mock trials, as well as, law dinners provided he/she paid for same. (3) Any aggrieved member of the Society shall have the right and access to the judicial council, hereinafter refer to as the Judiciary and shall have a right to fair hearing in all matters within its jurisdiction. (4) Every member shall, except as otherwise provided by this constitution, shall have right to cause of action in the Judiciary, compelling any member, officer or organ to conform with the aims and objectives set out in CHAPTER TWO of this Constitution. (5) Every member shall enjoy and have the right(s) as guaranteed under the constitution of the Federal Republic of Nigeria. Careful scrutinies of these provisions starting with the former, shows that persons regarded as members 

13 | Page  
of the Society are those who have been duly admitted and matriculated by the school. The provision of this SECTION is loud and clear. It brings with it no ambiguity or cloudy skies, it’s as clear as the sky in the month of February. It is trite law that when a court is faced with wordings of a statue that’s unambiguous and clear, it must apply the literal rule of interpretation. See NIGERIAN ARMY V ABUO (2022) 12 NWLR (Pt. 1844) 349. My Lords, let me quickly draw the attention of this honourable court to the UNIVERSITY OF ILORIN STUDENT HANDBOOK particularly RULE 15 which provides as follows; “Only students who have satisfied the minimum educational requirements of University of Ilorin are offered final admission and then matriculated as students of the University. On the matriculation day, each fresh student swears to the matriculation oath which shall be provided by the University (SEE APPENDIX B). Violation of the matriculation oath is a very serious offence which shall lead to disciplinary actions.” Matriculation according to the Black’s law Dictionary 8th Edition @ pg. 3102 as “the process of admission to membership as by examination or enrolment in a university or college”. Premised on this, is Matriculation a process of enrolment which comes to an end by the Oath taking Ceremony organized by the University of College. It is safe to say the there’s a huge difference between matriculated students and Newly Admitted Students. The provision of SECTION 5 of the LSS Constitution considers only duly admitted and matriculated students as MEMBERS OF THE LAW STUDENTS' SOCIETY. See the case of TAOFIK WALIU OPEYEMI V. THE ELECTORSL COMMISSION, LSS & OTHERS LSSJ/005/19. Before a member or an individual can enjoy the rights enlisted in SECTION 7 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION, he/she must have fulfilled the requirements in SECTION 5 above. The claimants’ argued this vehemently and the defendants also agreed by stating that “we do not dispute that Section 5 of the LSS Constitution is a condition precedent to Section 7 of the Same constitution”. Hence, being duly admitted is a condition precedent to been matriculated, while being duly Matriculated is a condition precedent to being a member of the Law students Society, University of Ilorin. Students who are yet to be matriculated are at best, newly admitted law students. This issue is hereby resolved in favour of the claimants.  ISSUE TWO: WHETHER OR NOT THE APPOINTMENT DOES FALL UNDER THE PURVIEW OF SECTION 7 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION? A careful perusal of SECTION 7 of the LSS Constitution shows that appointment to any post is not a right. Clearly, there isn’t any subsection as to appointment by the President of the Society or any other Executive member. It is therefore not a right guaranteed by the Constitution. Jurisprudentially, "right" denotes something that is due to a person by just claim, legal guarantee, or moral principle ...a legally enforceable claim that another will do or will not do a given act. See the case of OKO V AG EBONYI STATE (2021) 14 NWLR (Pt. 1795) 63 SC. If the appointment to the Career Advancement Office was a right, then every member of the society is entitled to be appointment. As best, such appointment is based on Privilege. See HABEEB V LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT & ANOR SUIT NO: LSSJ/009/21. Appointment by of the 2nd defendant by the 1st defendant doesn’t fall under the purview of SECTION 7 of the LSS Constitution. This issue is thereby resolved in favour of the defendants.  

14 | Page  
ISSUE 3: WHETHER OR NOT THE FIRST DEFENDANT HAS EXERCISED HIS POWER IN LINE WITH THE PROVISION OF LSS CONSTITUTION BY APPOINTING 2ND DEFENDANT AS A MEMBER OF CAREER ADVANCEMENT OFFICE. The defendants counsel argued that the 1st defendant exercised his power under SECTION 19(10) of the LSS CONSTITUTION in appointing the 2nd defendant as a member of the Career Advancement Office. And that assuming without conceding that Section 5 is a condition Precedent to Section 7, then Equity considers as done, that which ought to be done. I must say that the defendants’ counsel already admitted that Section 5 is a condition precedent of Section 7 of the LSS Constitution; I do not know why he is approbating and reprobating. No doubt, this court has given its view on the ability of the President of the Society to exercise his powers under SECTION 19 (10) of the LSS CONSTITUTION in the case of OKWARA & 1 ORS v THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT, L.S.S. &11 ORS CITATION: (2021) LCELR 2021001 stating “Hence, I vehemently believe that, by the inclusion of the provision of Section 19(10) the legislator does not intend to act in vain, and that the object is to expand the powers of the president to take further action outside what is contained in Section19(1) to (9) as may appear to him necessary, so long as it is incidental to the performance of his official duties and in fulfillment of the aims and objectives of the law pursuant Section 4 of the Law student’s society constitution, 2018, (as amended). See Section 10 (2) of the Interpretation Act, Cap192, LFN, 1990.” PER ABDULRAHEEM (JLSS) at Pg.12. My Lords, it is important to state that while interpreting the provisions of a Constitution or a Statute, in ascertaining the true meaning of the provisions, the Constitution or the statute being interpreted must be read as a whole and construed so. See the case of ONIKEKE MUSA AND 2 ORS V LIBERTY CHAMBERS (2019) LCELR 1819004 ; ACTION CONGRESS AC & ANOR VS INEC (2007) 12 NWLR (PART 1048) 222 at 259 and BUHARI & ANOR V. OBASANJO & ORS (2005) 13 NWLR (PT.941) 1 AT 219. Premised on this, the provision of SECTION 6 of the LSS CONSTITUTION cannot be ignored. It provides thus, “MEMBERS (bona fide in respect of the Society) means any member who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all rights and privileges indicated to the membership of the Law Students Society.” This provision introduces the word “privileges” which is not contained in SECTION 7 OF THE LSS CONSTITUTION. It therefore means that a member is entitled to enjoy the rights contained in SECTION 7 of the Constitution and also to any privilege which can accrue to him/her. This privilege would only be enjoyed if he/she is a BONA FIDE MEMBER IN RESPECT OF THE SOCIETY. The 1st defendant has used his power under SECTION 19(10) of the Constitution to appointment the 2nd defendant. The question is, is the 2nd defendant a Member of the Society by virtue of SECTION 5 & 6 of the LSS CONSTITUTION? The answer is NO. Notwithstanding the fact that “Equity considers as done that which ought to be done”, it is noteworthy to emphasize that “He who comes to Equity must come with clean hands” and “He who wants Equity must do Equity”. The 1st defendant has ignored the provision of SECTION 5 and 6 of the LSS CONSTITUTION, this court cannot then rely on the maxim if equity he is relying on. It might baffle one to ask that what exactly is the intention of the law makers in providing for 

15 | Page  
SECTIONS 5, 6 & 7 of the LSS Constitution? I believe the reason is not far fetched, it is basically to avoid the issue of appointing or voting students into positions when they have not been Matriculated. Unmatriculated students can still be moved to other faculties few days before the Matriculation. It is only after Matriculation that they become bona fide members of that Faculty or department. The intention of the lawmakers is not to discriminate against newly admitted students but it is channeled towards the growth of the Society. Hence, it cannot be disregarded. In the case of the court enlisted some features to guide a court while interpreting a constitution or statute, it held that ; a) In interpreting the Constitution, which is the supreme law of the land, mere technical rules of interpretation of statutes are, to some extent, inadmissible in a way so as to defeat the principles of government enshrined therein; b) as a corollary, all sections are to be construed together and it is not permissible to construe sections in isolation; c) where the words are clear and unambiguous, a literal interpretation will be applied, that is, they will be accorded their plain and (c) grammatical meaning; d) where there is inherent ambiguity in any section, a holistic interpretation would be resulted to in order to arrive at the intention of the of its framers; e) since the draftsperson is not known to extravagate words or provisions, a section of the Constitution should not be construed in such a manner as to render other sections redundant or superfluous; f) if the words of a statute are ambiguous, then the lawmaker’s intention must be sought, first, in the statute itself, then in other legislation and contemporary circumstances and by resort to the mischief rule; Therefore, the intention of the lawmaker cannot be waived. My Lords, May I reiterate that the Constitution of the Law Students’ Society, 2018(as) amended is Supreme and binding on all organs and members within the society as encapsulated in SECTION 1 OF the same Constitution. All provisions of this constitution are to be respected and followed in its entirety. The purpose of the Constitution would be defeated if some provisions are obeyed and some others are disregarded. There are plethora of cases in our jurisdiction that emphasized the supremacy of the constitution, there’s hardly any case that the court does not reiterate it. See ONIKEKE MUSA AND 2 ORS V LIBERTY CHAMBERS; OKWARA & 1 ORS v THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT, L.S.S. &11 ORS and HABEEB V LAW STUDENTS’ SOCIETY EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT & ANOR (supra). The latter case is more recent and the court thoroughly dealt with the issues before it, which is very similar with the one before this honourable court. Yet, the same action that was shunned in that case has repeated itself. Therefore, the appointment of the 2nd defendant by the 1st defendant as a member of the Career Advancement Office is unconstitutional. This issue is hereby resolved in favour of the claimants. JUSTICE OLAGUNJU AISHAT TIINUOLA   I had a preview of the lead judgment just delivered by my learned brother, JAMIU RIDWAN CJLSS, I adopt the facts and arguments of parties as contained in the lead judgment reached by 

16 | Page  
him. RESOLUTION In consolidation of the issues presented before the court by both parties, this case shall be resolved under the following issues:  1. WHETHER THE PROVISION OF SECTION 5 OF THE CONSTITUTION SERVES AS A CONDITION PRECEDENT FOR THE APPLICATION OF SECTION 7.  2. WHETHER THE APPOINTMENT OF THE 2ND DEFENDANT AS A MEMBER OF THE CAREER ADVANCEMENT OFFICE IS UNCONSTITUTIONAL.  ISSUE 1: The express provision of Section 5 of the Constitution is to the effect that only a duly admitted and matriculated student of the faculty of law is to be considered a bona fide member of the Law Student’s Society. Section 6 which serves as the interpretation section also provides unequivocally, that a member of the society is one who has been duly admitted and matriculated and is entitled to all the rights and privileges entitled to a bonafide member of the society. The rights guaranteed under Section 7 of the constitution are also those that are undeniably afforded to members of the society as defined by Sections 5 and 6 of the LSS constitution. Condition precedent as discussed by the counsels to both parties, was defined in the case of ADAM V AG LAGOS STATE (2020) 17 NWLR (Pt 1753) 281 where the court expressed that it is “a condition that delays the vesting of a right until the happening of an event.” It was also stated in the case of UNIVERSAL PROPERTIES LIMITED V PINNACLE COMM. BANK (2022) 12 NWLR (Pt 1845) 523 that a condition precedent is a “provision which makes the existence of a right dependent on the happening of an event. The right is then conditional as opposed to an absolute right”. The 3rd edition of the black’s law dictionary defined the word “matriculate” as “to enroll or enter in a register, specifically to enter or admit to membership, in a body or society, whereof a list was made”. Relying on the foregoing, I am of the opinion that the language of the constitution is very clear and unequivocal as to the issue of who is to be considered a member of the law student society as this has been provided for by virtue of Sections 5 and 6. Only a duly admitted and matriculated student of the faculty of law is to be considered a member of the LSS and by implication, only such a person can be allowed to enjoy the privileges incidental to such a membership. Just as the claimants asserted in their written address, the 2nd defendant as at the time of her appointment, was not yet a matriculated student of the university and as such, cannot be considered a bonafide member of the society. Reliance is placed on the case of TAOFIQ WALIU V LSS ELECTORAL COMMISSION & ORS (LSSJ/005/19) where the court held that by reason of Section 5 of the Constitution, “no person shall be qualified, even if they are from the 200 level or 300 level, 400 level or 500 level to be in the LSS House of Representatives if they are not members of the society.” On the literal interpretation approach, the court held in the case of AMASIKE V REGISTRARGEN, C.A.C (2006) 3 NWLR (pt 968) 462 that the “courts are enjoined to apply the literal interpretation of words where such words are used in a statute without any ambiguity”. See also, the case of OLUWABUKOLA V A.G LAGOS (2022) 2NWLR (pt.1815) 499 where the court commented on situations where application of the literal rule may lead to absurdity and stated that “the duty of the court generally, is to interpret the law and not to make it. This is achieved by the literal interpretation and it is only when this might 

17 | Page  
lead to absurdity that the court allowed to look to other rules of interpretation”. With reliance placed on all of the authorities cited above therefore, I am inclined to conclude that the rights and privileges guaranteed by Section 7 of the Constitution are only those afforded to the members of the Law students Society as defined by Sections 5 and 6 of the constitution and no other person. Thus, a student that is yet to be matriculated though already admitted, as far as the constitution is concerned can even be likened to an already engaged lady that is yet to be married. She cannot rely solely on the engagement to claim rights and privileges that are afforded to a duly married wife. I remain on the stand of employing the literal interpretation rule as it is evident that there is no absurdity or injustice that is to occur as a result of this. The word “matriculated” must have been included by the law makers in addition to “admitted” for the sole purpose of indicating that both are conjunctive and not disjunctive conditions as it seems to have been suggested by the counsel to the defendant in his written address that must be fulfilled to attain membership of the society. For this purpose, I hold that Section 5 of the constitution is indeed a condition precedent to the application of Section 7 of the constitution. If a person has not been admitted and matriculated, such a person cannot be considered to be member of the LSS and as such, cannot enjoy rights guaranteed exclusively to members. I hereby resolve this issue in favor of the claimants.  ISSUE 2: Counsel to the defendant argued that the 1st defendant who is the president of LSS is empowered by Section 19(10) of the constitution to make appointments that may be incidental to ensuring the proper administration of the society. They also argued that the appointment of the 2nd defendant was made by virtue of this section and not in relation to Sections 5 and 7 of the constitution. I might be inclined to agree that it may be reasonably asserted the appointment of the 2nd defendant into the career advancement office is not necessarily a right as guaranteed under Section 7 of the constitution. However, as suggested by virtue of Sections 5 and 6 of the constitution both rights and privileges available under the constitution should only be afforded to bonafide members of the society. I rely on the case of HABEEB V THE EXECUTIVE PRESIDENT, (LSSJ/009/21). The 3rd edition of the Black’s law dictionary defines the word “privilege” as “a particular and peculiar benefit or advantage enjoyed by a person, company or class beyond the common advantage of other citizens”. With regards to this therefore, I assert that it should not be considered unreasonable to conclude that the appointment in question, though not necessarily a right, is a privilege that is open to a select few primarily because they are members of the society. My opinion on this issue therefore, is that the President derives the powers on which he is relying to make this appointment from the constitution itself and as provided for under Section 1 of this same constitution, the constitution is supreme and binding on every member and body within the society. Any act done in violation of the supreme legal document shall be prima facie declared null and void to the extent of its inconsistency. This is just as was held by this court in the case of AKANNI & 2 ORS V THE WELFARE DIRECTOR OF LSS & 2 ORS. See also the case of TIMOTHY V OFORKA (2008) 9 NWLR (Pt. 1091) 204. The provision of Section 19 of the constitution cannot be divorced from the provisions of Sections 5 and 6 and cannot be exercised in isolation of same. Thus, it is my 

18 | Page  
opinion on this issue that the appointments made by the president of the society in exercise of his powers under Section 19 (10) of the constitution must be made in accordance to the provisions of the constitution and specifically Sections 5 and 6 in relation to this case. I therefore conclude that the appointment of the 2nd defendant, Miss Abdulrazaq Angel as a member of the career advancement office was in contravention of constitutional provisions and as such, void ab initio. I resolve this issue in favor of the claimants and hold that as at the time when this suit was filed, the 2nd defendant was not a duly matriculated member of the LSS and it would nothing short of an unreasonable injustice on all the bonafide members of the society to allow this status quo to subsist. I grant reliefs number 1, 2, 3, and 4 sought by the claimants and declare that Miss Abdulrazaq Angel ceases to be a member of the career advancement office with immediate effect.
